# Cycle 286: generalized-Kummer boundary and closure audit

## Scope

Cycle 284 recorded the following version-pinned external corollary:
Floccari--Fu `arXiv:2610.31740v1`, Theorem 1.4, composed with Foster
`arXiv:2512.04114v2`, Theorem 8.15, gives the rational Hodge conjecture in
every codimension for every self-power of one projective generalized-Kummer-
type irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifold of complex dimension `4d`.

This cycle tests the nearest formal enlargements of that statement and nothing
else:

1. mixed products of distinct covered varieties;
2. products with unrelated abelian varieties;
3. generalized-Kummer dimensions `4d+2`; and
4. direct-sum, disjoint-union, product, deformation, and motivic closure
   arguments proposed as bridges.

Four independent bounded audits were run: a mixed-product proof architect, an
odd-parameter primary-source auditor, a motivic-category formalizer, and a
hostile source referee. The owning core also checked the two version-pinned
primary texts directly. No route repair, publication, contact, or Clay claim
was authorized.

## The source theorem remains correctly matched

Let `K` be projective of generalized Kummer deformation type and
`dim_C K=4d`, with `d>=1`. Foster Theorem 8.15 proves his full Lefschetz
standard Conjecture 1.1 for `K`, in every required degree. In the convention of
Floccari--Fu, `dim_C K=2n`; hence `n=2d` is even and at least two. Their Theorem
1.4 then gives

```text
h(K) in <h(J_K)>_hom^tensor
and
HC_Q(K^r) for every r>=1 and every codimension,
```

where `J_K` is the third intermediate Jacobian and the motivic category uses
rational homological motives and algebraic correspondences. Foster v2's
specialized theorem is unconditional as stated; the contingency described in
the earlier Floccari--Fu Remark 1.5 is not a surviving hypothesis. No prime
condition is used in this composition.

The hostile source audit also checked the abelian input. The required statement
that every discriminant-one Weil-type abelian fourfold satisfies rational Hodge
on all self-powers is stated, for example, in Floccari's 2026 *Geometry &
Topology* Theorem 1.3 and Floccari--Fu `arXiv:2504.13607v2`, Theorem 1.2. It
must not be attributed merely to a generic-to-special argument from Markman or
to algebraicity of only the Hodge--Weil subspace.

Thus Cycle 284 needs no correction. The conclusions below are boundary walls,
not defects in that corollary.

## Exact algebraic-correspondence closure lemma

**Common-generator lemma.** Let `Y,X_1,...,X_s` be smooth projective complex
varieties. Suppose each rational homological motive `h(X_i)` belongs to the
thick rigid tensor subcategory generated by `h(Y)`, using algebraic
correspondences, and suppose the rational Hodge conjecture holds for every
power `Y^m`. Then it holds for every finite mixed product of the `X_i`, with
arbitrary repetitions.

Indeed, each `h(X_i)` is an algebraic-correspondence retract of a finite direct
sum of Tate twists of powers of `h(Y)`: duals can be replaced using motivic
Poincare duality, and thickness supplies direct summands. Tensoring the
retractions places the motive of a mixed product in the same form. A Hodge
class maps to a Hodge class on a finite sum of powers of `Y`, is algebraic there
by hypothesis, and maps back algebraically under the retraction.

More generally, if `h(X_i)` is generated by `h(Y_i)`, the same proof requires
the Hodge conjecture for all mixed products of the `Y_i`. Tensoring the
individual retractions produces mixed source products, not a disjoint list of
self-powers. Separate results for each family of self-powers are therefore not
enough for this proof.

This gives valid special cases beyond the literal Cycle 284 headline:

- repeated copies of one `K`, already covered;
- `K^a x J_K^b` for one fixed `K`;
- any additional factors algebraically motivated by the same `J_K`; and
- finitely many `K_i` whose intermediate Jacobians are all isogenous to one
  common `J` for which Hodge holds on every self-power.

These are common-generator consequences, not arbitrary mixed-product results.

## Mixed-product wall

For distinct covered varieties `K_i`, the source theorems give separately

```text
h(K_i) in <h(J_i)>_hom^tensor
and HC_Q(J_i^m) for every m,
```

but a mixed product is controlled by the jointly generated category

```text
<h(J_1),...,h(J_s)>_hom^tensor.
```

The missing objects are cross-Hodge tensors or, equivalently, off-diagonal
Hodge morphisms between tensor constructions from different `J_i`. Algebraic
control of each diagonal tensor category does not formally control these
off-diagonal blocks. Kunneth does not help: a Hodge class in
`H^a(K_1) tensor H^b(K_2)` need not be an external product of Hodge classes on
the individual factors. The `H^1 tensor H^1` component of the diagonal of an
elliptic curve is the elementary warning.

Consequently the implication

```text
[for every i,m, HC_Q(K_i^m)] => HC_Q(product_i K_i)
```

is unsupported by the cited theorems. This is a formal wall, not a
counterexample to Hodge for the mixed product.

Passing to Andre motivated motives does not remove the wall: a motivated
cross-correspondence is not automatically an algebraic correspondence. Nor
does semisimplicity prove fullness of algebraic realization on the jointly
generated category. Polarization identities would work only after fullness for
the direct-sum generator had already been established, which is the missing
claim.

## Disjoint-union, direct-sum, product, and deformation walls

For `Y=coprod_i X_i`, one has the exact open-and-closed decomposition

```text
Y^r = coproduct_(i_1,...,i_r) X_(i_1) x ... x X_(i_r).
```

Chow groups, Hodge classes, and cycle maps decompose componentwise. Therefore
`HC_Q(Y^r)` is equivalent to Hodge for every word of length `r` in the `X_i`;
in particular it already contains the desired mixed-product assertions.
Separate self-power results control only the constant-word components, so
applying an all-powers theorem to `Y` would assume the missing conclusion.
Moreover, a nontrivial disjoint union is not a connected irreducible
holomorphic symplectic manifold: it has `h^(0,0)>1` and `h^(2,0)>1`. A direct
sum `oplus_i h(K_i)` is a motive, not an admissible geometric input to either
source theorem.

A nontrivial product of the `K_i` is also not an admissible replacement: it has
`h^(2,0)>1` and, for `s` factors, `b_2=7s`, whereas a generalized-Kummer-type
IHSM has `h^(2,0)=1` and `b_2=7`. Projective bundles, blow-ups, and Hilbert
schemes do not package arbitrary factors while preserving this deformation
type.

Finally, deformation equivalence of separate fibers supplies neither an
identification of their rational Hodge structures nor deformation-invariance
of arbitrary algebraic representatives. Propagating a Hodge class through a
family requires a separate theorem and its hypotheses. Factors of different
dimensions cannot even be fibers of one smooth family of fixed relative
dimension.

## Unrelated abelian-factor wall

The motivic statement gives only

```text
h(K x A) in <h(J_K),h(A)>_hom^tensor.
```

Thus Hodge for all mixed powers `J_K^a x A^b` would suffice, but Hodge for
powers of `J_K` and powers of `A` separately does not formally provide it.

The unrestricted claim with arbitrary abelian `A` is especially strong. If
Hodge held for `K x A` for every abelian variety `A`, then Hodge would hold for
every such `A`: pull a Hodge class back under `K x A -> A`, represent it by an
algebraic cycle, and apply the refined pullback along `{k} x A`. The two cited
preprints do not prove the general Hodge conjecture for abelian varieties.

## The `4d+2` wall

Write `dim_C K=2n`. A generalized-Kummer target of complex dimension `4d+2`
has odd `n=2d+1`.

1. Foster Theorem 8.15 proves the full Lefschetz standard conjecture only in
   complex dimension `4d`, i.e. even `n`.
2. Foster's section 9 summary gives, for odd `n`, only the standard conjecture
   in degrees strictly below `n+1`, not in all degrees.
3. Floccari--Fu Theorem 1.4 itself assumes even `n`; therefore even a new full
   Lefschetz-standard result would not, by literal substitution alone, extend
   their theorem to odd `n`.
4. The prime branch supplies no odd `n>=2`: if odd `n` has prime `n+1`, then
   the even number `n+1` must be `2`, forcing the excluded case `n=1`.

There is a sharp representation-theoretic indication of where the known odd
range stops. Put `N=n+1`; for odd `n`, `N` is even. In Floccari--Fu Theorem
2.3, the order-two character summand first appears in cohomological degree

```text
2(N-N/2)=N=n+1,
```

exactly the first degree not covered by Foster's stated odd-`n` range.

Independently, the Floccari--Fu passage from the invariant motive to the full
motive uses even-`n` fixed-locus and canonical-cycle results. Their Theorems
2.2 and 2.5 are parity-restricted; the latter uses the integer exponent
`(N/e-1)/2`, which is unavailable for the relevant even quotients in the
odd-`n` case. Their dimension-unrestricted Theorem 2.1 controls only the
invariant motive `h(K)^(Gamma_N)`, not all of `h(K)`.

Hence the first missing input is not merely a cosmetic parity removal. One
needs both suitable full standard-conjecture input in the odd family and an
odd-`n` algebraicity/motivic-generation replacement for the non-invariant
summands. The six-dimensional `Kum^3` base case is separately known from
Floccari `arXiv:2308.02267v2`, Theorem 1.1; its statement must not be silently
upgraded to all self-powers, and it is not a family-wide consequence of the
Cycle 284 composition.
