# Cycle 208: asymmetric MCSP template and the disjoint-gadget sharing gate

Fix the fan-in-two basis `B={AND,XOR,NOT}` with constants, write `C_B(h)` for
minimum circuit size, and let `MCSP_(n,s)` accept the `N=2^n`-bit truth tables
of functions with `C_B(h)<=s`. This note isolates the exact finite lemma that
would transfer the selector-free `INDEX` pattern to MCSP, and tests the most
literal disjoint-subfunction implementation.

## Exact asymmetric template

Let `A subset [N]` have size `N/2`. For a row assignment `rho in {0,1}^A` and
a column assignment `sigma in {0,1}^([N]\A)`, write `rho cup sigma` for the
completed truth table.

**Asymmetric MCSP template `AMT(n,s,m)`.** For every balanced `A` there are
injectively indexed rows

\[
  rho_A(z),\qquad z\in\{0,1\}^m,
\]

and columns `sigma_A(1),...,sigma_A(m)` such that

\[
 C_B(rho_A(z)\cup sigma_A(j))\le s
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z_j=1.                 \tag{208.1}
\]

The polarity in (208.1) is immaterial. The word *asymmetric* records that a
construction needs explicit upper-bound circuits only on the `z_j=1` side and
a lower bound only on the `z_j=0` side; no pairwise hardness assertion among
all completions is needed.

**Finite template theorem.** If `AMT(n,s,m)` holds, then every exact
deterministic OBDD for `MCSP_(n,s)`, in every variable order, has midpoint width
at least `2^m`.

Indeed, take `A` to be the first half of the chosen order. The cut matrix on
the displayed rows and columns is exactly `K(z,j)=z_j`. Hence all `2^m` rows
are distinct residual functions. This is precisely the Cycle 206 HWB argument
with the semantic identity `HWB(alpha_z,beta_j)=z_j` replaced by the complexity
threshold identity (208.1).

The statement is finite and falsifiable for each `(n,s,m)`: enumerate balanced
sets, row and column maps, and all circuits of size at most `s`. A proposed
certificate is refuted by one completed table with the wrong side of the
threshold. This is not an efficient test and does not assert that the template
exists.

## Direct-sum rigidity sufficient for the template

Suppose a proposed construction supplies completed tables `T_A(z,j)` having
the required row/column consistency

\[
 T_A(z,j)|_A=rho_A(z),\qquad
 T_A(z,j)|_{[N]\setminus A}=sigma_A(j).                       \tag{208.2}
\]

The exact circuit lemma needed from the construction is the following
one-sided gap, uniformly over the unqueried bits:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 z_j=1 &\Longrightarrow C_B(T_A(z,j))\le U,\\
 z_j=0 &\Longrightarrow C_B(T_A(z,j))\ge L,
\end{array}
\qquad U<L.                                                    \tag{208.3}
\]

Any integer `s` with `U<=s<L` proves `AMT(n,s,m)`. Thus (208.2)--(208.3), not a
symmetric assertion such as "every cross-splice is hard," is the exact
selector-free MCSP target.

For a component construction, a useful auditable specialization is this.
Assume the query `j` exposes components with baseline costs `kappa_i`, and
there is a common overhead `b`. It is enough to prove

\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_B(T_A(z,j))&\le b+\sum_i kappa_i+e
     &&(z_j=1),\\
 C_B(T_A(z,j))&\ge b+\sum_i kappa_i+e+g
     &&(z_j=0)
\end{aligned}                                                  \tag{208.4}
\]

for one integer `g>=1`, despite arbitrary choices of every `z_i`, `i!=j`.
Equation (208.4) is the required **asymmetric direct-sum rigidity lemma**. Its
substantive content is that no circuit can share the baseline/component work
well enough to save the one-bit gap `g` on the hard side. Upper bounds on the
easy side may freely share gates.

A version with uncertain component bounds makes the required margin explicit.
If the easy construction costs at most

\[
 U_j=b_j^+ + u_j^1+\sum_{i\ne j}\max_b u_i^b
\]

and rigidity gives, on `z_j=0`,

\[
 L_j=b_j^- + l_j^0+\sum_{i\ne j}\min_b l_i^b,
\]

then a single MCSP threshold exists exactly when

\[
       \max_j U_j < \min_j L_j.                                \tag{208.5}
\]

This exposes a frequent hidden failure: uncertainty from the `m-1` unqueried
components consumes the queried one-bit gap. Equal-cost encodings of zero and
one, or a separate cancellation argument, are therefore part of the needed
lemma rather than harmless bookkeeping.

## Entropy window

Let

\[
 L_{n,r}=(r+1)(n+r+2)\bigl(3(n+r+2)^2\bigr)^r
\]

be the standard upper bound on the number of functions represented by
`B`-circuits of size at most `r`. If the `2^m` row words are generated by
distinct size-`r` circuit templates, then necessarily

\[
 m\le \lfloor\log_2 L_{n,r}\rfloor
   =O(r\log(n+r)).                                               \tag{208.6}
\]

There is also the trivial cut bound `m<=N/2`. Consequently the natural finite
search window for this implementation is

\[
 m\le \min\{N/2,\lfloor\log_2 L_{n,r}\rfloor\}.                \tag{208.7}
\]

The condition is only necessary: row assignments in the abstract template
need not themselves be complete easy functions, so (208.6) applies only to a
construction that encodes rows by easy circuit templates. At the MMW value
`r=N^(1/log^* n)`, this ceiling is `N^o(1)` state bits. Therefore even a perfect
direct-sum theorem inside this easy-template window would remain below the
fixed-power space target; an official transfer needs a construction outside
that ceiling or a different argument for the search relation and update time.

## Simplest disjoint-subfunction gadget

For `t=2^q`, let

\[
 D(f_1,\ldots,f_t)(a,x)=f_a(x),\qquad a\in\{0,1\}^q.
\]

The `t` restrictions to the disjoint address subcubes recover all components,
so restriction gives only

\[
 C_B(D(f_1,\ldots,f_t))\ge\max_i C_B(f_i),                     \tag{208.8}
\]

up to the chosen convention for fixing constants. It does not give a sum.

The strongest immediate counterexample takes `f_i=H` for every `i`. Then
`D(f_1,...,f_t)=H`, so all component copies share the entire circuit. Requiring
distinct components does not repair the problem. Let `x_1,...,x_t` be payload
variables and put

\[
 f_i(x)=H(x)\mathbin{XOR}x_i.
\]

If `c=C_B(H)`, then

\[
 c-1\le C_B(f_i)\le c+1.
\]

A binary multiplexer computes `x_a` with at most `3(t-1)` gates, using
`MUX(b,u,v)=v XOR (b AND (u XOR v))`. Therefore

\[
 D(f_1,\ldots,f_t)(a,x)=H(x)\mathbin{XOR}x_a
\]

has size at most

\[
 c+3(t-1)+1.                                                    \tag{208.9}
\]

The functions `f_i` are pairwise distinct, while their nominal component sum
is at least `t(c-1)`. Hence every proposed inequality

\[
 C_B(D(f_1,\ldots,f_t))\ge\sum_i C_B(f_i)-Delta
\]

must allow

\[
 Delta\ge (t-1)c-4t+2.                                         \tag{208.10}
\]

For finite sufficiently large payload dimension, counting supplies an `H`
with `c` much larger than `t`, so the loss in (208.10) is essentially the full
shared cost `(t-1)c`. This is a gate-sharing counterexample, not merely a
duplicate-component pathology.

## Verdict

The finite target is (208.2)--(208.3), with (208.4) the clean direct-sum form
and (208.5) the exact margin test. The plain disjoint-subcube multiplexer fails
that target: restrictions certify a maximum, while a common hard core can be
computed once and shared across every component. Any viable Cycle 208 gadget
must enforce anti-sharing by an additional syntactic model, a provable
information bottleneck, or component-specific transformations that rule out a
common core. Proving such rigidity for unrestricted circuits would itself be a
major new circuit lower bound. No MCSP lower bound, unrestricted lower bound,
or `P != NP` conclusion is claimed.
