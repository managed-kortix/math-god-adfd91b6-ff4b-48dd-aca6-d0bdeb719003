# Cycle 287: Kummer-Jacobian mixed-product classification

## Scope

Cycle 286 isolated one possible escape from its mixed-product wall: perhaps the
special structure of the intermediate Jacobians, together with the
low-dimensional classification of Hodge classes on abelian varieties, controls
all cross-Hodge tensors. This cycle tests exactly that possibility.

Four bounded and mutually hostile audits were used: a primary-source audit of
Moonen--Zarhin and the low-dimensional literature, a Tannakian/product-closure
analysis, a classification of generalized-Kummer intermediate Jacobians, and
an independent hostile referee. The owning core checked the decisive passages
in Moonen--Zarhin and Milne directly. No stopped PEL/graph construction,
publication, correspondence, or Clay claim was authorized.

The outcome has two parts:

1. the unrestricted mixed-product enlargement remains unsupported; and
2. a precise full-`SU` subcase does follow, including mixed products of covered
   generalized-Kummer varieties whose intermediate Jacobians have full expected
   Hodge group.

The second item is a derived product lemma, not a claim of novelty and not a
resolution of the Hodge conjecture.

## Exact structure of the intermediate Jacobian

Let `K` be projective of `Kum^n` deformation type, with `n>=2` and
`dim_C K=2n`. Then

```text
b_3(K)=8,   h^(3,0)(K)=0,   h^(2,1)(K)=h^(1,2)(K)=4,
```

so its third intermediate Jacobian `J_K` is an abelian fourfold. For an ample
line bundle `L`, write

```text
q_K(c_1(L))=2k>0.
```

O'Grady's Theorem 1.5 gives a Weil-type action by

```text
F_(K,L) = Q(sqrt(-2(n+1)q_K(L)))
        = Q(sqrt(-(n+1)k)),
```

where the equality is equality of fields, and the corresponding polarized
Weil fourfold has discriminant one. The unpolarized torus `J_K` depends on `K`,
whereas the displayed polarization and Weil action depend on `L`.

This has several important negative consequences.

- There is no common imaginary quadratic field: the field depends on the square
  class of `(n+1)k`, and Markman's complete families realize all possible
  imaginary quadratic fields.
- There is no universal simplicity statement. Special members can decompose;
  for an actual generalized Kummer variety `K^n(A)`, one has
  `J_(K^n(A)) ~ A x A-hat ~ A^2` up to isogeny.
- A finite product of the `J_K` has only simple isogeny factors of dimension at
  most four, by Poincare complete reducibility, but this is not a theorem about
  an abelian variety of total dimension at most four.
- Different `J_K` need not have independent Hodge groups. The varieties
  `K^n(A)` and `K^m(A)` already give coupled examples because both Jacobians
  are isogenous to `A^2`.

Floccari--Fu, Theorem 1.2 of `arXiv:2504.13607v2`, supplies the positive input

```text
HC_Q(J_K^r) for every r>=1,
```

because `J_K` is a discriminant-one Weil fourfold. Its quantifiers concern the
powers of one fixed fourfold, not arbitrary products of different fourfolds.

## Why low dimension does not close mixed products

Moonen--Zarhin, *Hodge classes on abelian varieties of low dimension*,
Theorem 0.1 assumes total dimension at most four, and Theorem 0.2 assumes total
dimension five. Neither applies to a product of two fourfolds merely because
its simple factors have dimension at most four.

Their section 3.1 states the exact obstruction. For abelian varieties `X_1`
and `X_2`, the projections

```text
Hg(X_1 x X_2) -> Hg(X_i)
```

are surjective, but they explicitly warn that

> `Hg(X_1 x X_2) != Hg(X_1) x Hg(X_2)`

can occur. They further state that this happens exactly when, for some `m,n`,
the Hodge ring of `X_1^m x X_2^n` is not generated by classes from the two
individual self-powers. At Lie-algebra level the failure is a graph over a
common quotient. These are precisely the missing cross tensors.

Thus

```text
[HC_Q(A^r) for all r] and [HC_Q(B^s) for all s]
```

does not formally imply `HC_Q(A^m x B^n)`. No counterexample to that
implication is known--one would disprove rational Hodge--but the implication is
not a Tannakian closure rule. Milne's neat/almost-neat examples likewise show
that separate divisor-generation properties need not survive products; they
do not provide nonalgebraic classes.

## Product-group criterion

The exact sufficient condition is elementary and useful.

**Lemma 287.1 (product Hodge groups).** Let `A_1,...,A_s` be complex abelian
varieties. Assume

```text
Hg(product_i A_i) = product_i Hg(A_i)
```

and assume rational Hodge for every self-power of every `A_i`. Then rational
Hodge holds for every mixed product `product_i A_i^(m_i)`.

**Proof.** Repetitions do not change a factor's Hodge group. Under the displayed
product equality, Kunneth and invariants under a product group identify each
mixed Hodge class with a sum of external products of Hodge classes on the
individual self-powers. Hodge-group invariants in a pure cohomological degree
have equal Hodge bidegrees, so odd-degree invariant summands vanish. The
individual classes are algebraic by hypothesis, and external products of
algebraic cycles are algebraic. `QED`

A sufficient group-theoretic hypothesis is that the connected reductive Hodge
groups have no common positive-dimensional quotient, including no common
central torus quotient. It is not enough to compare only derived groups. The
statement is cleanest for Hodge groups: Mumford--Tate groups retain a common
weight character.

Milne's Remark 1.18 is a literal published instance. For general polarized
Weil varieties with pairwise nonisomorphic groups `SU(phi_i)`, Goursat gives
product Hodge group; for determinant-one fourfolds he concludes Hodge for all
mixed powers. The words `general` and `pairwise nonisomorphic SU(phi_i)` are
hypotheses and may not be replaced by `pairwise nonisogenous` without a
separate argument.

## A stronger full-`SU` derived subcase

For fourfolds with full expected Hodge group, that separate argument is
available.

**Proposition 287.2 (mixed full-`SU` Weil fourfolds).** Let `A_1,...,A_s` be
abelian fourfolds of Weil type and discriminant one. Suppose that, for each
`i`,

```text
Hg(A_i) = SU(V_i,phi_i),
```

as subgroups in the natural rational representation
`V_i=H_1(A_i,Q)` (equivalently its dual `H^1`), where `V_i` is the
four-dimensional Hermitian space over the relevant imaginary quadratic Weil
field.  Thus this means equality with the full expected special unitary group
in its natural representation, not merely an abstract isomorphism of algebraic
groups. Then rational Hodge holds
for every mixed product

```text
product_i A_i^(m_i),    m_i>=0.
```

**Proof.** Put `W_i=H_1(A_i,Q)`, so that `W_i` is the natural rational
representation in the hypothesis.  It is irreducible.  Indeed, after extending
scalars to `Qbar`, the group becomes `SL_4` and

```text
W_i tensor Qbar = Std_4 direct-sum Std_4^dual.
```

The two constituents are nonisomorphic and are exchanged by the Galois action
of the imaginary quadratic Weil field.  Thus `W_i` is irreducible over `Q`.
Its commutant becomes `Qbar direct-sum Qbar` and therefore has rational
dimension two.  The imaginary quadratic Weil field `E_i` already embeds in
that commutant, so the commutant is exactly `E_i`.  More explicitly, central
weight homotheties do not change a commutant, and the standard identities give

```text
End^0(A_i) = End_HS(W_i) = End_(MT(A_i))(W_i)
           = End_(Hg(A_i))(W_i) = E_i.
```

It has no nontrivial idempotent, so `A_i` is simple up to isogeny. Consolidate
all isogenous factors: the original mixed product is isogenous to a product of
powers of one representative from each isogeny class, and rational Hodge is
invariant under isogeny by pull--push. Thus only pairwise nonisogenous
representatives remain.

Each `G_i=Hg(A_i)` is an absolutely almost simple `Q`-group of type `A_3`:
after extension to `Qbar` it is `SL_4`.  Let
`H=Hg(product_i A_i)` and put `h=Lie(H)`.  Then `h` is a semisimple
`Q`-subdirect Lie algebra of `direct-sum_i Lie(G_i)`.  Indeed, the image of the
solvable radical of `h` in every simple `Lie(G_i)` is a solvable ideal and hence
zero; the inclusion in the direct sum then kills the radical.  Write
`h=direct-sum_a s_a` as a sum of its simple ideals.  For every coordinate `i`,
exactly one `s_a` maps nontrivially, and hence isomorphically, onto
`Lie(G_i)`: at least one does by surjectivity, while two cannot because their
images commute.  The supports of the `s_a` therefore partition the coordinate
indices.  Consequently, if `h` is proper, one support has size at least two,
and every two coordinates in that support give the graph of a
`Q`-Lie-algebra isomorphism

```text
alpha: Lie(G_i) -> Lie(G_j).
```

This is the generalized Goursat statement needed here; it is stronger and more
precise than merely finding a graph after extending scalars.

After extension to `Qbar`, each `W_i` is `Std_4 direct-sum Std_4^dual` as
an `sl_4`-module. Every automorphism of `sl_4` preserves this unordered pair:
inner automorphisms preserve each constituent and the unique diagram
automorphism exchanges them.  Hence the following scalar-extension identity
has a nonzero right-hand side:

```text
Hom_(g_i)(W_i, alpha^*W_j) tensor_Q Qbar
  = Hom_(g_i,Qbar)(W_i,Qbar, alpha_Qbar^*W_j,Qbar) != 0.
```

Here `g_i=Lie(G_i)` and `alpha^*W_j` means restriction through the rational Lie
isomorphism `alpha`.  This equality, rather than a claim that one chosen
geometric intertwiner descends, is the rational descent: the Hom spaces are
kernels of `Q`-linear intertwining equations.  A nonzero rational intertwiner
therefore exists.  Lie invariance equals invariance under the connected joint
Hodge group, with no residual finite central-character obstruction.  Since the
two homology structures have the same weight, the common weight homothety acts
trivially on their Hom, so Hodge-group invariance is also Mumford--Tate
invariance.  The intertwiner is therefore a rational Hodge morphism. Full
faithfulness of `H_1` for abelian varieties up to isogeny turns it into a
nonzero homomorphism `A_i -> A_j` tensored with `Q`. Simplicity and equal
dimension make that map an isogeny, contradicting the consolidation step.

Therefore `h=direct-sum_i Lie(G_i)`.  Since `H` and `product_i G_i` are
connected closed subgroups with the same Lie algebra, `H=product_i G_i`.
Lemma 287.1 applies, because Floccari--Fu prove Hodge for
every self-power of each discriminant-one Weil fourfold. `QED`

Moonen--Zarhin section 3.1 supplies the two-factor graph framework, but their
Lemmas 3.3--3.4 are not being invoked verbatim.  Their uniqueness hypothesis
for a faithful irreducible rational length-one representation actually fails
in this trivial-discriminant outer `A_3` setting.  All three fundamental
weights of `A_3` are minuscule, and trivial discriminant lets the middle module
`L(omega_2)` descend to a six-dimensional rational quadratic representation
through the central isogeny `Spin_6 -> SO_6` (`A_3=D_3`).  The proof above does
not need uniqueness among arbitrary length-one modules: it uses the tailored
fact that the other weight-one factor is known to have geometric constituents
`{omega_1,omega_3}`, and every automorphism of `sl_4` preserves that unordered
pair rather than sending an endpoint to `omega_2`.

The discriminant-one hypothesis is used for the unconditional self-power Hodge
input, not for the Hodge-group independence argument. The proposition does not
assert that every discriminant-one Weil fourfold has full `SU`: special points
can have smaller semisimple groups, extra endomorphisms, decompositions, or CM
tori.

## Consequence for mixed generalized-Kummer products

Let `K_1,...,K_s` be projective generalized-Kummer-type IHSMs with

```text
dim_C K_i divisible by 4,
```

and let `J_i=J^3(K_i)`.  Floccari--Fu
`arXiv:2610.31740v1`, Theorem 1.4, proves that `K_i` is motivated by `J_i`
provided the Lefschetz standard conjecture holds for `K_i`.  Foster
`arXiv:2512.04114v2`, Theorem 1.7/8.15, proves that conjecture for every
projective generalized-Kummer-type IHSM of complex dimension `4d`.  Their
literal composition therefore gives, unconditionally in the displayed
range,

```text
h(K_i) in <h(J_i)>_hom^tensor,
```

where the right side is the thick rigid tensor subcategory of rational
homological motives generated by `h(J_i)` using algebraic correspondences.

We use the following transfer lemma explicitly.  If each `h(X_i)` lies in the
thick rigid tensor subcategory generated by `h(Y_i)`, then for any fixed
multiplicities the motive of `product_i X_i^(r_i)` is an
algebraic-correspondence split summand of a finite direct sum of Tate twists of
products `product_i Y_i^(m_i)` (with the exponents depending on the summand).
Indeed, by the definition of the thick rigid tensor subcategory, each
`h(X_i)` is a split summand of a finite direct sum of Tate twists of powers of
`h(Y_i)`; one then tensors these algebraic splittings.  In the present
application the relevant generated categories are semisimple as well, because
standard conjectures hold for abelian varieties. Thus, if rational Hodge holds for all mixed
products of the `Y_i`, a Hodge class on an `X_i`-product maps by algebraic
correspondences to an algebraic class on such a finite sum and maps back
algebraically under the splitting.  This is the common-generator/retract
argument from Cycle 286 with several generators.

Hence Proposition 287.2 gives the following exact derived corollary.

**Corollary 287.3.** If every `J_i` has full expected Hodge group
`SU(V_i,phi_i)` in its natural rational representation, then rational Hodge
holds in every codimension for every mixed product

```text
product_i K_i^(r_i),    r_i>=0.
```

No pairwise nonisomorphism hypothesis on the `SU` groups is required.  First
replace isogenous `J_i` by representatives `B_1,...,B_t` of the distinct
isogeny classes and put `J=product_a B_a`.  Every `h(K_i)` attached to a
repeated class is generated by the corresponding `h(B_a)`.  Moreover each
`h(B_a)` is an algebraic direct summand of `h(J)`: insert the origins in all
other factors to obtain a section of the projection `J -> B_a`.  Each `B_a` is
a simple discriminant-one Weil fourfold with full expected `SU`, and
Proposition 287.2 proves rational Hodge for every power `J^m` (equivalently,
for all mixed powers of the `B_a`).  This is exactly the input required by the
transfer lemma.

More generally, without any full-`SU` hypothesis, if all `J_i` are isogenous
to one common discriminant-one Weil fourfold `J`, then every `h(K_i)` is
generated by `h(J)`, and the common-generator lemma plus rational Hodge for all
self-powers of `J` proves the same mixed-product conclusion.  This precise
common-isogeny-generator case is separate from Corollary 287.3.

## Exact remaining wall at special points

For arbitrary discriminant-one Weil fourfolds, and therefore arbitrary
`J_(K_i)`, the full-`SU` proof does not apply. A special member can have extra
endomorphisms, a decomposition into lower-dimensional factors, a CM Hodge
group, or several common simple/toric quotients with another member. Neither
Moonen--Zarhin's total-dimension classification nor the separate self-power
theorems classify every such coupling or prove algebraicity of all resulting
higher cross tensors.

The first missing theorem is therefore exactly one of:

1. a classification showing that every joint special-point Hodge group either
   splits or reduces through algebraic isogeny/common-generator data; or
2. an algebraicity theorem for every cross tensor produced by the surviving
   common-quotient couplings.

The assertions `all simple factors have dimension <=4`, `the Weil fields are
the same`, or `the factors are nonisogenous` do not supply either theorem.
This is a literature/formal wall, not evidence that a mixed-product Hodge
class is nonalgebraic.

## Official boundary and disposition

Corollary 287.3 enlarges one externally covered special family from
self-powers to a full-`SU` mixed-product subfamily. It still does not approach
the official quantifier over all smooth projective complex varieties, and even
within generalized-Kummer type it excludes arbitrary special Jacobians and the
`4d+2` dimensions walled in Cycle 286.

- Record the full-`SU` mixed subcase and the exact special-point wall.
- Do not advertise it as new: the argument is a formal Hodge-group synthesis.
- Do not turn Markman's statement for abelian fourfolds themselves into an
  all-powers or arbitrary-product theorem.
- Do not reopen the stopped quartic-CM/PEL construction.
