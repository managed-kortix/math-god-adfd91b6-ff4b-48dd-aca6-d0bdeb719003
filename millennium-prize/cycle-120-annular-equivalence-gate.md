\[
 K_r=e^{-rH_\perp},\qquad
 \|K_rf\|^2\le q\|f\|^2

\[
 \inf\sigma(H_\perp)\ge-\frac{\log q}{2r}.
\]

\[

production theorem, not a consequence of OS convergence.

## Exact free calibration

In Gaussian lattice gauge theory, after gauge and harmonic-sector quotient,
the transverse mode energy is

\[
 E(k)=2\operatorname{arsinh}(\widehat k/2).
\]

For the smallest nonzero momentum on an `L^3` torus,

\[
 E_L=2\operatorname{arsinh}(\sin(\pi/L))
 =\frac{2\pi}{L}+O(L^{-3}).
\]

Gauge-invariant `SU(N)` quadratic boundary observables create color-singlet
two-gluon states and have Gaussian contraction `e^(-2rE_L)->1`.  Gauge
invariance removes the colored one-gluon state, not the zero two-particle
threshold.  This rigorously defeats volume-uniform contraction in the free
theory.

It does not defeat exact interacting Yang--Mills: a nonperturbative mass
`a^(-1) exp(-A/g^2)` is invisible to every finite perturbative order and to the
complete formal series.  A nonperturbative volume-uniform lower bound on the
soft-mode Rayleigh quotient would itself prove zero spectral threshold.

For the central `SU(2)` convolution calibration, high spins do not escape:
`I_(2j+1)(beta)/I_1(beta)` decreases with `j`.  The unresolved issue is the
interacting physical spectrum, not a Peter--Weyl tail in the product model.

The Yang--Mills annular funnel is therefore retired.  A fresh funnel must
produce a prior local coercive, compactness, or multiscale mechanism rather than
restate full-complement spectral contraction.  No Millennium result is claimed.
