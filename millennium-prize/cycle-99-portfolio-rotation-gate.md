# Cycle 99: structural portfolio gate

## RH: Sonine and canonical systems

Burnol's Sonine/co-Poisson spaces give an unconditional nested filtration and
unconditional completeness/minimality theorems for evaluators at all zeta
zeros.  Since zeros off the critical line are included, this completeness does
not imply RH.  The xi-dependent positive canonical realization for every shift
`omega>0` would imply RH, but extending it throughout that range is exactly the
Hermite--Biehler/zero-free assertion.  The unconditional Hilbert filtration and
the RH-producing positive Hamiltonian do not combine into a weaker production
lemma.

## BSD: rank-one closure

Analytic rank one already implies Mordell--Weil rank one and finite Sha by
Gross--Zagier--Kolyvagin.  What remains for all such curves is the exact strong
leading-term formula, equivalently every prime-primary valuation identity.  For
a fixed curve most primes are controlled, but the finite exceptional set can
include `2`, bad additive primes, reducible residual representations, and
integral congruence/Tamagawa factors.  Algebraic rank one alone does not imply
analytic rank one: by converse Gross--Zagier--Kolyvagin, the missing qualitative
input is precisely finiteness of Sha.  The obstruction is integral primary
control, not modularity or the real height formula.

## Hodge: monodromy fixed parts

Invariant cycles and semisimplicity identify and split monodromy-fixed Hodge
substructures, but their projectors need not be algebraic.  A boundary cycle is
vertical and does not restrict to nearby fibers unless it lies on a relative
Chow/Hilbert component dominating the base.  A quartic-K3 family transverse to
a Noether--Lefschetz divisor gives an exact warning: a central line class is
locally monodromy invariant in a smooth family but ceases to be `(1,1)` nearby.
If flat Hodgeness is imposed on every fiber, generic algebraicity becomes the
variational Hodge problem itself.

## Yang--Mills: finite subgroups

Finite subgroups cannot become uniformly dense in `SU(N)`.  Jordan's theorem
gives a dimension-dependent bounded-index abelian normal subgroup; a Hausdorff-
dense sequence would force connected nonabelian `SU(N)` to be a finite union of
cosets of one limiting abelian subgroup, impossible.  Even where finite
subgroups densify, as in `Z_q` inside `U(1)`, the unscaled cycle-Laplacian gap
goes to zero rather than the rotor gap.  Operator normalization and uniformity
in subgroup, volume, and lattice spacing remain indispensable.
