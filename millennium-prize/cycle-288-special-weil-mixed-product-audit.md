# Cycle 288: special Weil-fourfold mixed-product audit

## Scope

Cycle 287 proved the mixed-product statement for discriminant-one Weil
fourfolds with full expected special-unitary Hodge group and left one precise
question: can the same conclusion be extended to **all** special points?
This cycle tests that extension by classifying the rational Hodge-group
couplings that can occur, checking their exterior-power representations, and
auditing whether the cited motive theorems algebraize any new mixed tensors.

The target is deliberately bounded.  Let `B_1,...,B_r` be abelian fourfolds of
Weil type and discriminant one.  After consolidating common simple isogeny
factors, decide whether every proper joint Hodge group either

1. forces a weight-one Hodge morphism (hence an isogeny already handled by a
   common generator), or
2. produces only mixed tensors whose algebraicity follows from an audited
   theorem.

Four mutually hostile audits were used: simple/decomposable Hodge-group
classification, exact representation couplings, mixed-product literature, and
algebraic-motive transfer.  Exploratory lattice examples were subjected to
integral saturation and Hodge-minimality tests.  No publication, correspondence,
or Clay claim was authorized.

## First correction: what the individual theorem says

Floccari--Fu, Theorem 1.2, states that the rational Hodge conjecture holds for
**all powers of any** abelian fourfold of Weil type with discriminant one.  It
therefore covers every special point one at a time.  It does not say that for
unrelated `B_i` the Hodge conjecture holds for

```text
product_i B_i^(m_i).
```

This distinction is exact.  Moonen--Zarhin section 3.1 identifies failure of

```text
Hg(B_1 x B_2) = Hg(B_1) x Hg(B_2)
```

with the occurrence, on some mixed powers, of Hodge classes not generated by
classes from the two separate self-power rings.  Those new invariant tensors
need independent algebraic cycles.

The attribution must also remain precise.  Markman's later secant-sheaf work
prints algebraicity of Weil classes for fourfolds for all discriminants and all
imaginary quadratic fields, and hence ordinary Hodge for each fourfold.  The
source-audited **all-self-powers at discriminant one** input used here is
Floccari--Fu Theorem 1.2.  No checked Markman statement supplies all powers at
arbitrary discriminant or arbitrary mixed products.

## Rational classification framework

Fix an imaginary quadratic field `K`.  A relevant fourfold admits a unital
embedding

```text
K -> End^0(B)
```

with tangent multiplicities `(2,2)` and a compatible polarization of Weil
discriminant one.  The Hodge-group classification is rational; existence of a
specified discriminant-one polarization is an additional arithmetic filter.
No classification row below silently asserts that every rational action admits
such a polarization.

### Simple fourfolds

Moonen--Zarhin section 2.5(ii) leaves precisely Albert types III, IV(1,1), and
IV(4,1).

#### Type IV(1,1): `End^0(B)=K`

The balanced simple case has

```text
Hg(B) = SU_K(V,psi),
V_Qbar = Std_4 direct-sum Std_4^dual.
```

This is the full outer `A_3` group, not a determinant-torus case.  After common
isogeny factors have been removed, a nontrivial semisimple graph coupling with
another `A_3` factor must use the whole adjoint `A_3`.  Every automorphism of
`A_3` preserves the unordered endpoint pair `{Std_4,Std_4^dual}`.  The graph
therefore gives a nonzero rational weight-one Hodge morphism and hence an
isogeny, contradicting consolidation.  This reproduces the Cycle 287 closure
for the simple IV(1,1) row.

#### Type III: definite quaternion multiplication

Moonen--Zarhin Theorem 0.1(ii) gives

```text
Hg(B) = Sp_D(V,phi),
```

an outer rationally almost-simple `D_2` form.  Over `C`,

```text
V_C = 2 (2 box-times 2),
```

while over `R` the adjoint factors are one split `A_1` and one compact `A_1`.
Abdulali's explicit Hodge circle is active on the noncompact factor and trivial
on the compact factor; the two `D_2` vertices are exchanged by rational
Galois descent.

The following minimality lemma is decisive.

**Lemma 288.1 (projected Hodge circle).**  Let `G=Hg(V)`, let
`h:S^1 -> G_R` be its Hodge circle, and let `pi:G -> Q` be a connected rational
quotient.  The rational Zariski closure of `pi h(S^1)` is `Q`.

**Proof.**  If its closure were a proper rational subgroup `Q'`, then
`pi^(-1)(Q')` would be a proper rational subgroup of `G` containing the Hodge
circle, contradicting the defining minimality of the Hodge group. `QED`

Thus the compact geometric `A_1` cannot by itself be a common rational quotient:
it is Hodge-inactive, and in any event it is not Galois-stable.  A rational
coupling must graph the whole `D_2` packet.  Comparing the natural weight-one
modules then forces a Hodge morphism and, for simple equal-dimensional
fourfolds, an isogeny.  Hence the tempting one-`A_1` Hom-free coupling is not
Hodge-realizable for nonisogenous simple type-III fourfolds.

Compactness alone is **not** the argument: CM Hodge tori are compact over `R`
and are generated by nontrivial projected Hodge circles.  Lemma 288.1 excludes
zero-projection quotients, not compact quotients as a class.

#### Type IV(4,1): degree-eight CM field

Here `End^0(B)=F` is a CM field of degree eight containing the balanced Weil
field `K`, and the Hodge group is a CM torus; in the relevant balanced case
Moonen--Zarhin use

```text
SU_(F/K) = ker(N_(F/K): U_F -> U_K).
```

Common rational torus quotients and joint cocharacter relations can occur, and
must be classified by Galois-stable saturated character sublattices rather than
by isolated complex characters.  This is no longer an algebraicity gap for a
product all of whose simple factors are CM: van Hoften--Sempliner Theorem I
(`hexagon:2610.00259v2`, 18 October 2026) proves rational Hodge for every
complex abelian variety admitting complex multiplication.  A finite product
of CM factors is itself such an abelian variety, so all of its mixed tensors
are algebraic.  The theorem does **not** cover a non-CM factor merely because
its Hodge group has a central torus or a common toric quotient.

The explicit cyclic-octic CM graph constructed during the audit is a useful
general warning but is **outside** this row.  A cyclic octic CM field has a
unique quadratic subfield, fixed by complex conjugation and therefore totally
real.  It contains no imaginary quadratic field, so its simple CM fourfold is
not of Weil type at all.  In any event its Hodge classes are now covered by the
CM theorem just cited; it cannot witness the residual non-CM toric wall.

### Decomposable fourfolds

Write

```text
B ~ product_i X_i^(m_i)
```

with pairwise nonisogenous simple `X_i`.  The correct block condition is

```text
K -> M_(m_i)(End^0(X_i))
```

for each isotypic block, with total signature `(2,2)`.  It is generally false
that `K` must act on each individual copy.

The dimension partitions give the following fail-closed framework.

- **`3+1`.**  The exceptional case is `E_K x T`, where `E_K` is a CM elliptic
  curve and `T` is a simple threefold with `K` in its endomorphism algebra.
  Moonen--Zarhin section 5.3 gives the genuine fiber products

  ```text
  {(u,g): u det_K(g)=1}
  ```

  when `End^0(T)=K`, and

  ```text
  {(u,t): u N_(F/K)(t)=1}
  ```

  for a sextic CM field `F`.  This is an actual determinant/norm coupling with
  no `H^1(E,T)`.  It produces the internal mixed Weil space in
  `H^1(E) tensor H^3(T)`, and Moonen--Zarhin describe the Hodge ring of this
  one fourfold.  It is not a theorem about arbitrary cross-products of two
  such fourfolds.  If two use the same `K`, their elliptic factors are already
  isogenous and must first be consolidated.

- **`2+2`.**  For two distinct simple surfaces, admissible balanced actions are
  constrained by the low-dimensional Albert list; semisimple graph couplings
  are controlled by the Moonen--Zarhin uniqueness lemmas and force matching
  simple factors.  For a square `S^2`, the field may embed only in
  `M_2(End^0(S))`; `Hg(S^2)=Hg(S)` acts diagonally.  Repetition is expected
  common-generator data, not a new exceptional quotient.  CM surface supports
  still require torus rather than semisimple arguments.

- **`2+1+1`.**  After consolidation the relevant pattern is `S x E^2`.
  Distinct singleton elliptic blocks carrying the same `K` would both have CM
  by `K` and hence be isogenous.  Semisimple matching is low-dimensional;
  remaining toric relations still need their own audit.

- **`1+1+1+1`.**  After consolidating elliptic supports, the Hodge group is the
  product over nonisogenous supports.  Products of elliptic curves have
  divisor-generated Hodge rings, so their mixed tensors are algebraic.

This framework closes the proposed semisimple escape.  Products whose entire
simple support is CM are now covered by van Hoften--Sempliner.  What remains is
not a pure-CM algebraicity problem but the classification of central-toric
cross-relations involving at least one non-CM special factor.

## Exact representation warning and why it does not realize the missing case

Over an algebraic closure let `U,X,Y` be two-dimensional standard
representations and put

```text
H = (SL(U) x SL(X) x SL(Y)) / <(-1,-1,-1)>,
V_A = (U tensor X) tensor M_A,
V_B = (U tensor Y) tensor M_B,
```

where `M_A,M_B` are trivial two-dimensional spaces.  Then

```text
Hom_H(V_A,V_B)=0,
```

but

```text
Lambda^2 V_A = 1
                 direct-sum 3 Sym^2 U
                 direct-sum 3 Sym^2 X
                 direct-sum (Sym^2 U tensor Sym^2 X),
```

and analogously with `Y`.  Therefore

```text
dim (Lambda^2 V_A tensor Lambda^2 V_B)^H = 1 + 3*3 = 10.
```

One line is the external product of polarizations; nine are genuinely mixed
Casimir contractions through `Sym^2 U`.  This proves exactly that

```text
no H^1 Hom  does not imply  no higher mixed invariant
```

for abstract symplectic representations.

It is not an abelian-variety counterexample.  The shared one-`A_1` quotient is
not rationally available for a genuine simple type-III Hodge group, by the
outer-`D_2` descent and Lemma 288.1.  If such a joint Hodge datum did exist, the
Casimir would be a `(2,2)` class in

```text
H^2(A) tensor H^2(B) subset H^4(A x B),
```

whose algebraicity would require a class in `CH^2(A x B)_Q`.  After multiplying
by `L_A^2`, it can equivalently be represented as a degree-preserving kernel in
`CH^4(A x B)_Q`.  Separate self-power theorems do not construct this bridge.

All exploratory `C_4` character-lattice toys are retracted.  Integral
saturation collapses their nominal rank-three torus to rank two, on which the
two weight-one multisets coincide; the proposed wedge relation therefore
already factors through degree-one intertwiners.  They supply no Hom-free
geometric model.

## What the available product and motive theorems do prove

1. **Milne, Remark 1.18.**  For general polarized Weil varieties with pairwise
   nonisomorphic `SU(phi_i)`, Goursat gives the product Hodge group.  For
   determinant-one fourfolds this yields Hodge for all mixed powers.  Neither
   `general` nor the nonisomorphic-group hypothesis may be removed.

2. **Hazama, as quoted by Moonen--Zarhin Theorem 3.2.**  Condition (D) is
   preserved under products with explicit no-type-IV or CM hypotheses.  This
   is genuine product closure, but condition (D) is stronger than self-power
   Hodge and does not cover arbitrary Weil/type-IV mixtures.

3. **Abdulali, Theorem 3.4.**  Domination transfer assumes the joint group has
   already split.  His published correction says the older unrestricted
   proposition is false; the corresponding theorem needs at most one simple
   type-III factor, and the CM theorem needs linearly disjoint CM fields.  These
   results cannot be used to infer splitting for the cases at issue.

4. **Floccari--Fu, Corollary 5.8 and Remark 5.9.**  Products inside one
   associated OG6 package are covered.  The checked cross-package statement
   assumes Hodge similarity of the **entire** transcendental K3-type
   structures, which implies isogeny of the associated Weil fourfolds.  A
   shared rank-three or `A_1` constituent is not enough.

5. **Common-generator transfer.**  If all motives are algebraic-correspondence
   retracts of tensor constructions from one smooth projective `Y`, and Hodge
   holds for all powers of `Y`, then it holds for all their mixed products.
   This covers isogenous repetitions.  Taking `Y=product_i B_i` is circular,
   because Hodge for all powers of that `Y` is the mixed assertion itself.

6. **van Hoften--Sempliner, Theorem I.**  If a complex abelian variety admits
   complex multiplication, the Hodge conjecture holds for it.  The statement
   explicitly permits a CM algebra that is a product of CM fields, so it
   covers arbitrary mixed products of CM abelian factors.  This removes the
   pure-CM part of the toric wall, but not central-torus quotients shared with
   non-CM special factors.

Absolute-Hodge or Andre-motivated mixed tensors do not close the gap: motivated
is not the same as algebraic.  O'Sullivan distinguished cycles choose canonical
representatives for numerical classes of cycles already known to be
algebraic; they do not provide surjectivity from all Hodge classes.  Kimura
finite-dimensionality lifts already algebraic projectors/inverses modulo
nilpotence; it does not turn a merely Hodge-theoretic cross morphism into an
algebraic correspondence.  Specialization likewise handles transported
classes, not new Hodge classes appearing only after the joint Hodge group drops.

## Result and exact residual theorem

The cycle closes every proposed **semisimple** special-point coupling after
common isogeny factors are removed:

- full `A_3` graphs force isogeny;
- the type-III one-`A_1` graph is not a rational Hodge quotient, while a full
  `D_2` graph forces isogeny;
- low-dimensional decomposable semisimple graphs reduce to common supports;
- products supported only on elliptic curves are divisor-generated.

It does **not** close the arbitrary special-point mixed-product theorem.  The
first missing result is now narrower and explicit.

> **Residual non-CM central-torus product theorem.**  After removing common
> simple isogeny factors and separating the all-CM products already covered by
> van Hoften--Sempliner, classify every common rational torus quotient
> compatible with the paired Hodge cocharacters of discriminant-one Weil
> fourfolds and involving at least one non-CM factor--in particular the central
> parts of decomposable `3+1`, `2+2`, and `2+1+1` cases.  Then either prove
> that these quotients split or come from common algebraic generator data, or
> construct algebraic cycles for all mixed invariants they create.

This is a genuine algebraicity wall, not evidence that any Hodge class is
nonalgebraic.  The individual self-power theorem remains intact.  The Cycle 287
full-`SU` mixed-product corollary remains intact.  No extension to all special
points, no new theorem claim, no publication, and no Clay claim follows.
