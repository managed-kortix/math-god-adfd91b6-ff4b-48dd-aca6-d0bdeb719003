# Cycle 193: rigorous derivative certificate for `D=-1499`

## Result

Let

\[
 E:y^2+xy=x^3+1
\]

be `433a1`, and let `E^(-1499)` be its quadratic twist. Its conductor and root
number are exactly

\[
 N=972951433=433\cdot1499^2,\qquad w=-1.
\]

The verifier proves, using integer newform coefficients and directed rational
arithmetic only, that

\[
 \frac{425055458712371550288205049784482504359146782438892}{10^{50}}
 < L'(E^{(-1499)},1)
 <
 \frac{425182303658182754137303934460410663840644112448588}{10^{50}}.
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{L'(E^{(-1499)},1)>0.}
\]

No binary floating-point value occurs in the interval replay or verifier
output. The interval is deliberately wider than a numerical evaluation: its
purpose is a short, auditable nonvanishing proof once the mathematical and
coefficient inputs below are accepted.

## Trust boundary

The Python verifier is a fail-closed rational-arithmetic replay, not a complete
formalization of every input. It pins the coefficient CSV by SHA-256, enforces
its exact shape and normalization, proves the `pi`/`alpha` enclosures, checks
all directed interval invariants, pins the expected partial/tail/final integer
endpoints, and requires the final lower endpoint to be positive. Ordinary
Python `assert` statements are not used for certificate decisions, so `python
-O` cannot disable these checks.

The following remain outside that verifier's trust boundary:

- the mathematical derivation of the odd-sign approximate functional equation
  (193.1);
- identification of the pinned integers as the newform coefficients of the
  stated twist, generated by PARI's exact `ellan` recurrence;
- the conductor and exact root number, checked separately by the Cycle 192
  PARI verifier;
- the correctness of Python's arbitrary-precision integer and `Fraction`
  arithmetic and the SHA-256 implementation.

Accordingly, the script alone proves a positive interval for the series in
(193.1) with the pinned coefficients. The analytic-rank conclusion also uses
the stated mathematical identity, coefficient identification, and exact root
number.

## Exact approximate functional equation

Write

\[
 L(E^{(-1499)},s)=\sum_{n\geq1}a_n n^{-s}
\]

with integer coefficients `a_n`, and put

\[
 \alpha=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt N},\qquad
 E_1(x)=\int_x^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}t\,dt.
\]

Splitting the Mellin integral for the weight-two newform at `1/sqrt(N)` and
using `w=-1` gives the exact identity

\[
 L'(E^{(-1499)},1)=2\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{a_n}{n}E_1(\alpha n).       \tag{193.1}
\]

Indeed the two halves are exchanged by the Fricke involution. Their values at
the center cancel, while differentiating the factors `n^-s` and the incomplete
gamma integral leaves (193.1). Equivalently, this follows directly by
differentiating the standard smoothed functional equation at its odd center.

Here the normalization is explicit.  The elliptic curve corresponds to the
primitive normalized weight-two newform

\[
 f(z)=\sum_{n\geq1}a_n e^{2\pi i n z},\qquad a_1=1,
\]

of exact level `N`, and

\[
 \Lambda(s)=N^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(s),\qquad
 \Lambda(s)=-\Lambda(2-s).                                  \tag{193.1a}
\]

If

\[
 H(s)=N^{s/2}\sum_{n\geq1}{a_n\over(2\pi n)^s}
             \Gamma(s,\alpha n),
\]

then splitting at `1/sqrt(N)` gives `Lambda(s)=H(s)-H(2-s)`.
At `s=1`, the derivative of `Gamma(s,x)` is

\[
 \left.\partial_s\Gamma(s,x)\right|_{s=1}
 =e^{-x}\log x+E_1(x).
\]

Since `x=alpha*n=2*pi*n/sqrt(N)`, its logarithmic term cancels
exactly against the derivatives of `N^(s/2)(2*pi*n)^(-s)`.  Also
`Lambda'(1)=sqrt(N)*L'(1)/(2*pi)`, because `L(1)=0`.  These identities
give (193.1), including its factor `2` and the argument `2*pi*n/sqrt(N)`.
No squarefreeness of `N` enters this Mellin/Fricke calculation: `N` is the
arithmetic conductor (the exact newform level), not its radical.

The exact root number is already recomputed by the Cycle 192 PARI verifier.
The Cycle 193 generator uses PARI's exact `ellan` recurrence to write the first
`M=100000` integer coefficients.  PARI defines `ellan(E,M)[n]` to be the
coefficient `a_n` of the elliptic-curve L-function, equivalently the `n`-th
Fourier coefficient in the displayed normalized newform; it is not the
unitary coefficient `a_n/sqrt(n)` and has no index shift.  The generator checks
`a_1=1`, as well as the two bad-prime values `|a_433|=1` and `a_1499=0`.
The resulting CSV has SHA-256

```text
806f69693e70a187fab6d86a966e99ba352b76bfc8a8abc8d24692f125fe9828
```

and the Python verifier pins this digest.

## Rational enclosures

Machin's identity

\[
 \pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239)
\]

together with alternating rational Taylor bounds proves the strict enclosure

\[
 \frac{20143470058089910442088524096749042725}{10^{41}}
 <\alpha<
 \frac{20143470058089910442088524096749042727}{10^{41}}.          \tag{193.2}
\]

The verifier checks (193.2) after squaring against rational lower and upper
bounds for `4*pi^2/N`; it does not assume a decimal square root.

For each endpoint in (193.2), `exp(-alpha/(2K))`, with `K=16`, is enclosed by
the alternating exponential series on `[0,1]`. All later exponential values
are obtained by directed fixed-point interval multiplication at denominator
`10^50`. The function

\[
 u\longmapsto e^{-\alpha u}/u
\]

is positive and convex. Thus composite midpoint sums on the mesh `1/K` are
lower bounds for each unit-cell integral, while composite trapezoid sums are
upper bounds. Summing the unit cells backwards encloses every `E_1(alpha*n)`.
At `u=M+1`, the remaining integral uses the elementary rational bounds

\[
 \frac{e^{-\alpha u}}{\alpha u+1}
 \leq E_1(\alpha u)\leq
 \frac{e^{-\alpha u}}{\alpha u}.                              \tag{193.3}
\]

Monotonicity in `alpha` and sign-aware multiplication by each exact `a_n`
then give

\[
 S_M=2\sum_{n\leq M}\frac{a_n}{n}E_1(\alpha n)
\]

inside the rational interval

\[
 \left[
 \frac{425055634725652900855942081984698787572090256053427}{10^{50}},
 \frac{425182127644901403569566902260194380627700638834053}{10^{50}}
 \right].                                                     \tag{193.4}
\]

## Infinite tail

The bound used here really does hold for every `n`, including integers
divisible by the bad primes.  At a good prime `p`, Deligne's theorem and the
degree-two Euler factor give

\[
 |a_{p^r}|\leq(r+1)p^{r/2}.
\]

At `433 || N` the reduction is multiplicative, so the local factor is
`(1-a_433*p^(-s))^(-1)`, with `|a_433|=1`, and hence
`|a_(433^r)|=1`.  At `1499^2 | N` the reduction is additive and the local
factor is `1`, so `a_(1499^r)=0` for every `r>=1`.  Multiplicativity therefore
gives, without omitting either bad prime,

\[
 |a_n|\leq\prod_{p^r\Vert n}(r+1)p^{r/2}=d(n)\sqrt n.          \tag{193.4a}
\]

Thus the elementary divisor-pairing bound `d(n)<=2*sqrt(n)` gives
`|a_n|<=2n`. From (193.3), for
`q=exp(-alpha)`,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|2\sum_{n>M}\frac{a_n}{n}E_1(\alpha n)\right|
 &\leq4\sum_{n>M}E_1(\alpha n)\\
 &\leq \frac{4e^{-\alpha(M+1)}}{\alpha(M+1)(1-e^{-\alpha})}\\
 &<\frac{176013281350567737032200216283212943473614535}{10^{50}}.
                                                                    \tag{193.5}
 \end{aligned}
\]

The last inequality is evaluated outward with the lower endpoint for `alpha`
and rational exponential intervals. Combining (193.4) and (193.5) proves the
displayed final interval and nonvanishing.
