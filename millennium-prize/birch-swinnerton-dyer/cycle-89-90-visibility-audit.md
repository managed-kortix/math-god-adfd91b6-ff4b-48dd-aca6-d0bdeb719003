# Cycles 89--90: prescribed visibility audit

For an inclusion `E -> A` with quotient `C=A/E`, the visible subgroup is

\[
\ker(H^1(\mathbf Q,E)\to H^1(\mathbf Q,A))
=\operatorname{im}(C(\mathbf Q)\xrightarrow{\delta}H^1(\mathbf Q,E)).
\]

A visible class lies in `Sha` precisely when its quotient point lifts locally
to `A(Q_v)` at every place. Modular congruence produces a common residual
subgroup, but prescribed-class visibility also requires an actual rational
quotient point, local Kummer compatibility, and failure of global lifting.

The broad auxiliary-level theorem is now known: under substantial squarefree,
non-CM, residual-surjectivity, and local ramification hypotheses, Tamiozzo proves
that every class in `Sha(E/Q)[p]`, `p>3`, is visible in a quotient of a raised
modular Jacobian. The construction chooses as many admissible primes as the
relevant Selmer dimension and builds a basis by an invertible localization
matrix.

A proposed class-sensitive absolute bound of two auxiliary primes does not
follow. Each local switch changes Selmer defect by at most one, and abstract
bipartite reciprocity admits passive Selmer summands invisible to every shallow
Kolyvagin class. Even when admissible localization functionals separate the
whole Selmer space, all reciprocity classes can remain in one active line. A
positive shallow-generation theorem would require an independent full-rank
reciprocity matrix, which is precisely the missing generation input.

The explicit `1246B/1246C`, `p=5` visible subgroup `(Z/5Z)^2` is also published
by Cremona--Mazur. It is suitable only for a provenance-labelled software
reproduction, not a novelty claim.

Thus visibility remains meaningful arithmetic geometry but supplies neither a
new bounded theorem here nor the complex leading-term bridge required for BSD.
The sparse prescribed-class tactic is retired. No BSD result is claimed.
