# Cycle 277: hostile scaling and smooth-jet audit of CEB

## Verdict

`CEB` is not refuted by amplitude/time scaling or by a smooth initial jet.
For each fixed smooth datum its normalized left side tends to zero as
`tau -> 0`.  A datum-dependent family can compress any already existing
excursion to arbitrarily small physical time, but it cannot manufacture the
required excursion: by the exact `L^3` identity, crossing the `7/8` budget is
exactly the same as amplifying the `L^3` cube by a factor at least eight.

The one-split proposal nevertheless has a genuine proof wall.  Its permitted
inputs contain no scale-sensitive estimate capable of controlling positive
quartic pressure work by cubic viscosity and a fixed cubic endpoint reserve.
This retires `C276-CEB-ONE-SPLIT`; it does not prove `CEB` false.

## The budget is algebraically the target

Put

\[
 X(t)=\|u(t)\|_3^3,\qquad
 Q(u)=\mathcal P(u)-\nu\mathcal D(u).
\]

For every smooth solution,

\[
 3\int_0^\tau Q(u(t))\,dt=X(\tau)-X(0).             \tag{277.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
 3\int_0^\tau Q\,dt\geq {7\over8}X(\tau)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 X(\tau)\geq8X(0).                                  \tag{277.2}
\]

The pressure terminology does not create an independent budget after time
integration: viscous payment has already been included in the exact change of
`X`.  At a first running maximum, failure of `CEB` is precisely a factor-two
velocity-`L^3` excursion.  Thus an exact counterfamily would itself be a family
of smooth Navier--Stokes solutions with that amplification.  No such family is
derived here.

Conversely, if `CEB` holds at every first running maximum, continuity gives
`X(t)<8X(0)` throughout every smooth lifespan.  Up to the strict endpoint,
`CEB` is therefore the desired factor-two a priori bound rewritten through
the identity, not a weaker local pressure lemma.

## Exact amplitude, frequency, and time scaling

Let `(v,q)` solve periodic Navier--Stokes with viscosity `mu`, and let `N` be a
positive integer.  For `A>0`, define

\[
 u_{A,N}(x,t)=A v(Nx,ANt),\qquad
 p_{A,N}(x,t)=A^2q(Nx,ANt),                          \tag{277.3}
\]

where

\[
 \mu={\nu N\over A}.                                \tag{277.4}
\]

Then `(u_{A,N},p_{A,N})` solves the equation with viscosity `nu`.  Periodic
cell replication gives the exact laws

\[
 X_u(t)=A^3X_v(ANt),
 \quad \mathcal P(u(t))=A^4N\mathcal P(v(ANt)),
 \quad \mathcal D(u(t))=A^3N^2\mathcal D(v(ANt)).   \tag{277.5}
\]

After `s=ANt`, both sides of `CEB` acquire the same factor `A^3`:

\[
 3\int_0^\tau(\mathcal P(u)-\nu\mathcal D(u))dt
 =A^3\,3\int_0^{AN\tau}
       (\mathcal P(v)-\mu\mathcal D(v))ds.           \tag{277.6}
\]

Hence the normalized budget and the first-maximum condition are invariant.
Fixing `mu` and taking `A=nu N/mu` sends the corresponding physical time
`tau=s/(AN)` to zero like `N^{-2}`.  It follows that:

* any one counterexample at any positive scaled time would yield
  counterexamples at arbitrarily small physical times for viscosity `nu`;
* scaling alone supplies no counterexample, because the factor-eight ratio in
  (277.2) is invariant;
* a claim uniform over all smooth data cannot dismiss small `tau` merely by
  pointwise continuity, since the data norms grow along this family.

This is the exact answer to the small-time question: arbitrarily small times
are possible conditionally on one genuine factor-eight excursion, but no
amplitude/time rescaling turns a smaller excursion into such an excursion.

## Smooth initial jets

For a fixed smooth datum `u_0` with `X_0>0`, smooth dependence gives

\[
 X(t)=X_0+3Q(u_0)t+O_{u_0}(t^2),                    \tag{277.7}
\]

and therefore

\[
 {3\int_0^tQ(u(s))ds\over X(t)}
 ={3Q(u_0)\over X_0}t+O_{u_0}(t^2)\longrightarrow0. \tag{277.8}
\]

Thus pressure work cannot consume `7/8` of the endpoint budget at arbitrarily
small times for one fixed smooth datum.  If `Q(u_0)>0`, sufficiently short
times are first excursions, but their normalized budget is still `o(1)`.

Large initial jets do not change this conclusion.  Under (277.3),

\[
 Q_{\nu}(u_{A,N}(0))
 =A^4N\bigl(\mathcal P(v_0)-\mu\mathcal D(v_0)\bigr),\qquad
 X_{u}(0)=A^3X_v(0).                                \tag{277.9}
\]

The logarithmic first derivative can therefore grow like `AN`, while the
Taylor-valid physical window shrinks on the same scale.  Writing
`s=ANt` reduces the expansion back to that of `v`.  Extrapolating a large
positive first derivative until it predicts a factor-eight increase is not a
valid jet argument; it requires order-one control of the rescaled trajectory.

There is also an exact sign symmetry at the level of the pressure functional:
the pressure generated by `-w` equals that generated by `w`, while

\[
 \mathcal P(-w)=-\mathcal P(w),\qquad
 \mathcal D(-w)=\mathcal D(w).                       \tag{277.10}
\]

Thus any datum with nonzero pressure work has an orientation with positive
pressure work.  Amplitude scaling then gives

\[
 \mathcal P(Aw)=A^4\mathcal P(w),\qquad
 \nu\mathcal D(Aw)=\nu A^3\mathcal D(w),\qquad
 X(Aw)=A^3X(w).                                      \tag{277.11}
\]

This is an exact sign/scaling counterfamily to any proposed *pointwise*
universal charge of positive pressure work by `nu D+C X`: whenever
`P(w)>0`, its left side is quartic and its right side cubic.  It is not a
counterfamily to integrated `CEB`, because the nonlinear time scale is
`A^{-1}` and (277.1) still forces (277.2).

## First wall in the one-split mechanism

The first wall has already appeared before the high-amplitude region is
estimated.  With `A=X(tau)^(1/3)`, the constant-one `L^2` pressure estimate and
Young's inequality leave on the low-amplitude region

\[
 {A\over4\nu}\int_0^\tau\|u(s)\|_4^4\,ds.           \tag{277.12}
\]

Even assigning the whole permitted reserve to (277.12) requires

\[
 \int_0^\tau\|u(s)\|_4^4\,ds
 \le {7\nu\over6}X(\tau)^{2/3}.                      \tag{277.13}
\]

The frozen split is homogeneous under amplitude scaling and supplies no time
or derivative scale.  The maximum condition `X(s)<=X(tau)` controls only the
cubic norm; concentration at fixed `L^3` leaves `L^4` unbounded.  Recovering
`L^4` from dissipation introduces another spacetime quantity and therefore the
same unavailable scale-sensitive trajectory estimate.

Equation (277.11) separately rules out bypassing this gap by a pointwise charge
with a universal cubic remainder.  Time integration could close it only
through trajectory information strong enough to control the order-one
rescaled evolution; by (277.1), the requested final strength is exactly the
factor-eight bound.  None of the permitted one-split inputs provides that
information.

Return:

`CEB-SPLIT-WALL: FIRST UNCHARGEABLE TERM IS
X(TAU)^(1/3)/(4 NU) TIMES THE SPACETIME L4^4 NORM ON THE LOW-AMPLITUDE CHAIN;
THE REQUIRED BOUND (277.13) IS AN UNAVAILABLE SCALE-SENSITIVE TRAJECTORY
ESTIMATE.`

This is a proof-method wall only.  `CEB-FALSE` would require an actual smooth
first excursion satisfying (277.2), which this audit does not produce.
