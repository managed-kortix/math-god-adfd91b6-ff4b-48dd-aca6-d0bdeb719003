# Cycle 81: analytic-scale sparsity audit

The established geometric criterion uses signed component superlevel sets at a
successor of an escape time. Analyticity supplies radius

\[
r\asymp\sqrt{\nu/\|\omega\|_\infty},
\]

and one-dimensional sparsity at that scale allows a harmonic-measure argument
to prevent growth. The missing step is production of sparsity from the
equations.

An exact integral-geometric lemma is available. If

\[
{|E\cap B(x_0,r)|\over|B(x_0,r)|}\le\eta,
\]

then some centered diameter through `x_0` has occupied fraction at most
`eta^(1/3)`. The exponent and constants are sharp.

However, standard bounds do not supply the needed local volume fraction.
Chebyshev at the analytic scale requires smallness of the scale-invariant ratio

\[
{\|\omega\|_2^2\over\|\omega\|_\infty^{1/2}},
\]

or the corresponding `L^1` ratio. Energy controls only the time integral of
enstrophy. Across dyadic vorticity levels, the energy costs have weights
`2^(-k/2)` and are summable, so an infinite cascade can retain a non-small
critical ratio at finite total cost.

Energy-level negative-norm geometry reaches only the coarser scale
`Omega^(-2/5)`, while analyticity reaches `Omega^(-1/2)`; their ratio diverges
as `Omega^(1/10)`. Radius or threshold optimization cannot remove this gap.

Exact shear/Beltrami solutions and localized broad vorticity cores show that
analyticity, divergence freedom, energy, and maximum-point calculus do not
kinematically force sparsity. CKN theory controls spacetime concentration at
actual singular points, not fixed-time precursor superlevel geometry.

Thus the conditional sparsity criterion is preserved, but the proposed
automaticity mechanism is retired. No regularity or blowup result is claimed.
