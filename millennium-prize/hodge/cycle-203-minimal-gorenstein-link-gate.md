# Cycle 203: minimal codimension-three Gorenstein links retain the graph obstruction

## Bounded linkage family

Let

\[
A_0=E_i^3\times E_i^3,\qquad P=\operatorname {diag}(1,1,1,1,1,3),
\]

and let `G` be either `Gamma_I` or `Gamma_D`, where
`D=diag(3,1,1)`. Fix `L=mP`. This note tests the smallest non-complete-
intersection Gorenstein replacement for the Cycle 202 complete-intersection
link: a height-three ideal `I_W` generated by the five submaximal Pfaffians of
a `5 x 5` alternating matrix

\[
M=(s_{ab}),\qquad s_{ab}\in H^0(A_0,L),\qquad 1\leq a<b\leq5.       \tag{203.1}
\]

The five Pfaffians are sections of `2L`. The parameter scheme of centers
containing `G` is the quadratic incidence

\[
\mathcal P_G=
\{M:\operatorname {Pf}_a(M)|_G=0\ (1\leq a\leq5)\}.              \tag{203.2}
\]

The link locus is the open part on which the Pfaffian ideal has height three,
is minimal globally, and has no generic component in common with `G` beyond
the prescribed containment. Thus (203.2), followed by this explicit open
condition, parameterizes all homogeneous one-step links in the fixed system
`L`. It is finite dimensional for every fixed `m`.

There is also a useful exact affine atlas. Modulo `I_G`, condition (203.2) says
that the alternating matrix has rank at most two. On the pivot chart
`s_12 != 0`, put, for `3 <= a < b <= 5`,

\[
n_{ab}=s_{12}s_{ab}-s_{1a}s_{2b}+s_{1b}s_{2a}.                    \tag{203.3}
\]

Then `n_34,n_35,n_45` belong to `I_G`, and conversely these three conditions
reconstruct every matrix on this chart. After skew row-column operations the
localized Pfaffian ideal is

\[
(\operatorname {Pf}_1,\ldots,\operatorname {Pf}_5)_{s_{12}}
=(n_{34},n_{35},n_{45})_{s_{12}}.                                \tag{203.4}
\]

If `x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)` are graph-normal equations and
`(n_34,n_35,n_45)^t=A x+O(I_G^2)`, proper linkage is the open condition that
`det(A)` is nonzero at the generic point. Its residual ideal is locally

\[
I_R=(n_{34},n_{35},n_{45}):I_G
   =(n_{34},n_{35},n_{45},\det A).                                \tag{203.5}
\]

The ten choices of pivot cover the proper-link locus. The rank-zero boundary
`M|_G=0` has all Pfaffians in `I_G^2`, hence `A=0`; it is not a proper direct
link and is removed by the open condition. This explains why a globally
minimal five-Pfaffian center is nevertheless a complete intersection near the
generic point of the linked graph.

## K-class and degree-six Chern character

The Buchsbaum--Eisenbud resolution is

\[
0\to O(-5L)\to O(-3L)^5\to O(-2L)^5\to O\to O_W\to0.             \tag{203.6}
\]

Let `delta_(G,j)` be the three graph-divisor classes cutting out `G`. The
canonical liaison object

\[
E_G=I_G/I_W\simeq\omega_R\otimes\omega_W^{-1}
\]

is a rank-zero sheaf supported on the residual. Its exact K-class is

\[
\boxed{
[E_G]=1-5[O(-2L)]+5[O(-3L)]-[O(-5L)]
      -\prod_{j=1}^3(1-[O(-\delta_{G,j})]).}                      \tag{203.7}
\]

The terms of Chern-character degree below six vanish, and expansion of
(203.7) gives

\[
\boxed{\operatorname {ch}_3(E_G)=5L^3-[G]=[R].}                  \tag{203.8}
\]

In particular, the K-class and `ch_3` do not depend on any entry of `M`; the
matrix changes the residual scheme and the untraced obstruction, but not its
fundamental cycle. Since `P^3` has zero exceptional Weil projection,

\[
P_W\operatorname {ch}_3(E_{\Gamma_I})=-P_W[\Gamma_I]\ne0,
\qquad
P_W\operatorname {ch}_3(E_{\Gamma_D})=-3P_W[\Gamma_I]\ne0.       \tag{203.9}
\]

Thus both liaison objects are rank-zero and exceptional in degree six.

## First PEL obstruction

The center class `5m^3P^3` is horizontal on the full nine-dimensional PEL
base. Semiregularity applied to (203.8) therefore makes the first obstruction
of the residual the negative of the graph obstruction. For
`B in M_3(C)`, with `Q=diag(1,1,3)`, the two exact maps are

\[
\rho_I(B)=Q^{-1}B^t-B,
\qquad
\rho_D(B)=Q^{-1}B^t-DB.                                         \tag{203.10}
\]

Exact elimination gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
G&\dim\ker\rho_G&\operatorname {rank}\rho_G\\ \hline
\Gamma_I&3&6\\
\Gamma_D&1&8.
\end{array}                                                       \tag{203.11}
\]

The semiregularity map is injective on these graph-obstruction images by the
Cycle 152 calculation. Consequently every object (203.7), for every proper
matrix in (203.2), has raw Atiyah-obstruction rank at least `6` or `8`,
respectively. No choice of Pfaffian entries can produce rank zero.

This also gives the requested exact saturation certificate. Let `C_G` be the
constant coefficient matrix of the semiregularity obstruction (203.10), let
`J_0` be the ideal generated by its entries in the coordinate ring of
`mathcal P_G`, and let `Delta` denote the product of the height-three,
minimality, pivot, and `det(A)` open conditions. Rank zero requires `J_0` to
vanish. Since `C_G` contains a nonzero rational constant,

\[
\boxed{J_0=(1),\qquad (J_0:\Delta^\infty)=(1).}                  \tag{203.12}
\]

Hence the rank-zero locus is empty even before saturation. This is stronger
than a failure on a generic matrix: it excludes the entire minimal homogeneous
one-step Gorenstein-link family in the fixed polarization-divisor system.

## Outcome

Minimal Pfaffian linkage does produce genuinely new residual support and an
explicit rank-zero sheaf with nonzero exceptional `ch_3`. It does not produce
a rank-zero *obstruction map*. The universal K-class forces the same first PEL
obstruction as the linked graph, with ranks `6` and `8`; the exact rank-zero
parameter ideal saturates to the unit ideal.

This no-go is bounded to homogeneous `5 x 5` Pfaffian centers with all entries
in one fixed system `|mP|`. Weighted Buchsbaum--Eisenbud matrices whose center
class has a nonhorizontal exceptional contribution are not covered. Such a
weighted center would have to cancel the graph contraction already in its
universal K-class before matrix parameters could matter. No Hodge-conjecture
result is claimed.
