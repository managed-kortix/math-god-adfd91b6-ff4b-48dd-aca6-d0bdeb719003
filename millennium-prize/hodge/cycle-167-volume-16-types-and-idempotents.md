# Cycle 167: volume-16 types, two-isogeny transitions, and idempotents

The bounded locus `Z_16` has four possible restricted polarization types, but
it need not have four irreducible components.  Exact type is locally constant
in a smooth family of polarized abelian subvarieties and can change only when
one changes the subvariety or crosses through the ambient Hilbert incidence.
At the integral level, completely adapted polarized 2-isogenies give an
explicit directed transition graph among all four types.  Two genuinely
distinct threefold factors force extra rational idempotents, but not always a
strictly smaller locus than `Z_16`: one threefold already has a complementary
factor up to isogeny.

## Classification and count

For a polarization of type `(d_1,d_2,d_3)`, with

\[
 d_1\mid d_2\mid d_3,
 \qquad d_1d_2d_3=16=2^4,
\]

write `d_i=2^{a_i}`.  Then

\[
 0\leq a_1\leq a_2\leq a_3,
 \qquad a_1+a_2+a_3=4.
\]

The four partitions of 4 into at most three nondecreasing parts are

\[
 (0,0,4),\quad(0,1,3),\quad(0,2,2),\quad(1,1,2).
\]

Hence the exact list is

\[
 \boxed{(1,1,16),\ (1,2,8),\ (1,4,4),\ (2,2,4).}
\]

This counts polarization types, not embedded subthreefolds.  A fixed sixfold
can contain finitely or infinitely many subthreefolds of a given type.  Indeed,
for a fixed abelian subvariety `Y subset A`, the translates of `Y` form a
positive-dimensional family unless one requires passage through zero; even
subgroups through zero can be infinite at highly decomposable CM points.

## Exact prime-two transition rule

Let `(Y,N)` have type `d=(d_1,d_2,d_3)`, let

\[
 g:Y\longrightarrow Y'=Y/D
\]

have kernel a maximal isotropic subgroup `D subset Y[2]`, and let `N'` be the
descended polarization normalized by

\[
 g^*N'=2N.
\]

Then `|D|=2^3`, so `chi(N')=chi(N)`.  On a symplectic lattice `Lambda` for
`N`, if `K subset Lambda/2Lambda` is the subspace corresponding to `D`, the
target lattice and form are exactly

\[
 \Lambda'=\Lambda+\frac12\widetilde K,
 \qquad E'=2E.
\]

The target elementary divisors are the alternating Smith invariants of
`E'|Lambda'`.  This is the exact integral transition algorithm; isotropy of
`K` is both necessary and sufficient for integrality.

Enumerating the three-dimensional isotropic subspaces of the six-dimensional
binary module gives the following exact multiplicities.  An entry is the
number of kernels `D` producing the column type.

| source / target | `(1,1,16)` | `(1,2,8)` | `(1,4,4)` | `(2,2,4)` | total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `(1,1,16)` | 180 | 15 | 0 | 0 | 195 |
| `(1,2,8)` | 192 | 228 | 12 | 3 | 435 |
| `(1,4,4)` | 0 | 240 | 180 | 15 | 435 |
| `(2,2,4)` | 0 | 960 | 240 | 195 | 1395 |

The totals also follow without enumeration.  Modulo two, an elementary block
`d_iJ` is nondegenerate exactly when `d_i` is odd.  Thus the alternating form
has ranks `4,2,2,0` in the four rows.  Counting maximal isotropic
three-spaces for these four binary presymplectic forms gives respectively
`195,435,435,1395`.

The table is an abstract polarized-threefold calculation.  It does not assert
that every row entry extends to a `Z[i]`-stable Lagrangian kernel in the ambient
sixfold, or that it remains in the fixed determinant-`-3` PEL component.  Those
are additional ambient compatibility conditions.  At odd primes the restricted
volume 16 is a unit, so a completely adapted prime-`p` lattice change preserves
the 2-primary discriminant module and hence preserves the exact type.  Thus
type changes in the split-prime chains of Cycle 166 can occur only at the bad
prime two; the good split odd-prime examples retain `(2,2,4)`.

## The exact idempotent algebra lemma

**Lemma.** Let `A` be an abelian variety over an algebraically closed field and
let `Y,Z subset A` be abelian subvarieties.  Put `D=End^0(A)`.  Then:

1. there are idempotents `e_Y,e_Z in D` with images `Y,Z`;
2. `Y cap Z` is finite if and only if `e_Ye_Z=0` after choosing `e_Y` to have
   kernel a complement containing `Z`;
3. if `dim Y+dim Z=dim A` and `Y cap Z` is finite, addition
   `Y times Z -> A` is an isogeny and
   
   \[
    D\simeq End^0(Y\times Z)
      =\begin{pmatrix}
        End^0(Y)&Hom^0(Z,Y)\\
        Hom^0(Y,Z)&End^0(Z)
       \end{pmatrix};
   \]
4. if `Y` and `Z` belong to distinct simple isotypic factors, their two central
   support idempotents are independent and `Q times Q` embeds in the center of
   `D`;
5. if `Y` and `Z` are isogenous simple factors, the algebra generated by the
   two corner endomorphism algebras and a pair of inverse quasi-isogenies
   between the factors is `M_2(End^0(Y))`; the two projectors themselves
   generate only the diagonal copy `Q times Q` and are not central in this
   matrix block.

**Proof.** Poincare complete reducibility supplies an abelian complement
`Y'` and an isogeny `u:Y times Y' -> A`.  The conjugate

\[
 e_Y=u\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}u^{-1}
\]

is an idempotent in `End^0(A)` with image `Y`; similarly for `Z`.  If
`Y cap Z` is finite and the dimensions sum to `dim A`, addition is an isogeny,
so conjugating endomorphisms by it gives the displayed matrix algebra and the
orthogonal projectors `e_Y+e_Z=1`, `e_Ye_Z=e_Ze_Y=0`.  Conversely, orthogonal
projectors have finite intersection of their images.  The final two assertions
are the standard decomposition of the semisimple isogeny category into
nonisogenous simple isotypic blocks.  In the isogenous case, quasi-isogenies
give off-diagonal matrix units `e_12,e_21`; together with the two corner
endomorphism algebras, these generate the full `2 by 2` matrix block.  This
also proves the qualifications in item 2.  QED.

There is a useful converse formulation.  For any idempotents `e,f in D`, the
four corner spaces

\[
 eDf,
 \quad eD(1-f),
 \quad (1-e)Df,
 \quad (1-e)D(1-f)
\]

are the rational Hom spaces between the corresponding image and kernel
factors.  In particular, `e` and `f` commute exactly when the off-diagonal
Peirce components `ef(1-e)` and `(1-e)fe` of the element `f` vanish (and
equivalently with `e,f` interchanged).  If they commute, the
four elements

\[
 ef,
 \quad e(1-f),
 \quad(1-e)f,
 \quad(1-e)(1-f)
\]

are pairwise orthogonal idempotents summing to one; the nonzero ones give the
common isogeny refinement of the two decompositions.  This is the exact sense
in which a second *independent* factor can force a finer special locus.

## Does a second factor force a smaller locus?

Not without an independence condition.  Every point of `Z_16` already has a
nontrivial rational projector and, by Poincare reducibility, some complementary
abelian threefold.  Requiring merely "two abelian threefold factors" can
therefore add no condition at all; the complement need not have volume 16, but
the algebraic decomposition is automatic.

Even requiring two volume-16 threefolds does not by algebra alone force a new
projector type: the two may coincide, be complementary projectors arising from
the same decomposition, or move inside one repeated isotypic matrix block.  At
the Cycle 151 seed, the diagonal and antidiagonal are complementary and both
have type `(2,2,4)`.  More generally, `B^2` contains many graph threefolds while
its rational endomorphism algebra is the matrix algebra already forced by the
repeated factor.  The numerical requirement on the complement can nevertheless
cut some components of `Z_16`; this is a geometric question, not a consequence
of abstract idempotent algebra.

A genuinely smaller candidate special locus arises after fixing discrete
projector data not generated by one decomposition.  On a fine level cover, one
may require two subgroup families `Y,Z` of volume 16 and a specified
intersection dimension; these conditions have a proper Hilbert-incidence
model.  Alternatively one may fix integral endomorphisms realizing a
nontrivial Peirce pattern, such as commuting projectors with at least three
nonzero common summands; the fixed-tensor condition is a closed
endomorphism/Hodge locus.  Merely asking that some unspecified off-diagonal Hom
be nonzero gives a countable union of such loci and should not be called one
closed locus without a bounded integral realization.  Every fixed locus is
proper on a PEL component whose geometric generic endomorphism algebra is
`K=Q(i)`, since a field has no nontrivial idempotent at all.  To prove strict
containment in `Z_16`, however, one still needs one point of each relevant
component of `Z_16` whose endomorphism algebra has only the single forced
projector block.  Generic simplicity proves properness in the ambient PEL
space, not this relative strictness inside every component of `Z_16`.

Thus the exact answer is:

\[
 \boxed{\text{two factors imply extra specialization only when their
 projector algebra is strictly larger than that forced by one factor.}}
\]

This refines the bounded-locus obstruction but does not produce generic Hecke
transport or a Hodge-conjecture result.
