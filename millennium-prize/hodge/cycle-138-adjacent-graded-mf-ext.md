# Cycle 138: rigorous graded extension space for adjacent plane factorizations

The grading ambiguity left by Cycle 137 can be resolved exactly at the matrix-
factorization level.  Two adjacent plane Koszul factorizations have a
two-dimensional homogeneous odd morphism cohomology group in the specified
internal degree, with explicit full `4|4` cocycles.  This is a categorical input
for the next cone-lifting gate, not a Hodge or Chow result.

In homogeneous coordinates put

\[
L=V(q,r,s),\qquad M=V(p,r,s),
\]

so the planes meet in the line `P^1_(z0,z1)`.  Since the degree-33 potential
vanishes on both,

\[
F=pqA+rB+sC,
\qquad \deg A=31,\quad\deg B=\deg C=32.
\]

Use the three-generator presentations

\[
L:(q,r,s),\quad (pA,B,C),
\]

\[
M:(p,r,s),\quad (qA,B,C).
\]

Their exterior Koszul factorizations have shifted modules

\[
K^0=S\oplus S(-31)^3,
\qquad
K^1=S(1)^3\oplus S(-30),
\]

and full parity ranks `4|4`.  Direct sparse-polynomial multiplication verifies

\[
D_1D_0=D_0(33)D_1=F I_4.
\]

After applying the complete hypersurface resolution and taking degree zero, the
relevant complex is

\[
N_0\longrightarrow N_1^3
\longrightarrow N_2^3\oplus N_{33},
\qquad N\simeq k[z_0,z_1,q].
\]

Its kernel is

\[
\{(a,0,0):a\in N_1\},
\]

and the preceding image is the one-dimensional subspace generated by `q`.
Therefore

\[
\boxed{
H^1_{\mathrm{hom},0}\simeq N_1/kq
\simeq kz_0\oplus kz_1.
}
\]

Explicit full odd cocycles are

\[
\Phi_a=a\,\varepsilon_1-Aa\,\iota_1,
\qquad a=z_0,z_1.
\]

Their matrix entries have the required heterogeneous degrees `1` and `32`, and
both complete matrix-factorization cocycle equations hold.  This explains why
uniform-entry constant/linear searches can miss the actual graded extension
space.

The safe conclusion is internal to `HMF^gr(F)`: the specified homogeneous
morphism cohomology has dimension two.  No claim is made that a preferred
Orlov-window representative is literally a geometric extension of the plane
sheaves.  For degree 33 in six variables, Orlov's Gorenstein parameter is `-27`
in the standard convention; identifying the precise geometric images requires
explicit truncation, sheafification, and mutation bookkeeping.
