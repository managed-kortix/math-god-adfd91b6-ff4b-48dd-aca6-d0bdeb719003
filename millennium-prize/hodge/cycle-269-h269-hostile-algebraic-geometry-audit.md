# Cycle 269: hostile algebraic-geometry audit

## Terminal output

`DG-ALPHA-WALL`.

The first failed frozen-model requirement is the demand for a principal
generator of a pulled-back diagonal Cartier ideal on the declared chart
pairs. In fact none of the four pairs `U_c x U_d`, with `c,d in {Z,Y}`,
trivializes that ideal. The diagonal is a regular Cartier divisor on every
pair, but its invertible ideal is not principal. Thus the frozen two-open
cover cannot support the stipulated chartwise length-three scalar Koszul
model.

## Checks before the wall

Let

\[
 E:Y^2Z=X^3-XZ^2,\qquad U_Z=D_+(Z),\qquad U_Y=D_+(Y).
\]

The curve is smooth in characteristic zero. Therefore `E^3` is smooth of
dimension three, and its diagonal in `E^3 x E^3` is a regular closed immersion
of codimension three. Its conormal bundle is `Omega^1_(E^3/K)`, so the abstract
codimension and local-complete-intersection claims pass.

The two declared opens cover `E`: if `Y=Z=0`, the cubic equation forces
`X=0`, which is not a projective point. Both opens are affine, with the printed
coordinate rings

\[
 K[x,y]/(y^2-x^3+x),\qquad K[a,b]/(b-a^3+ab^2).
\]

Their intersection is principal affine. Hence all finite intersections of the
product cover are affine, and ordinary Cech acyclicity for quasi-coherent
sheaves on this cover is not the obstruction.

## Exact chart-pair census

Put

\[
 A=K[x,y]/(y^2-x^3+x),\qquad U_Z=\operatorname{Spec} A,
\]

and also put

\[
 B=K[a,b]/(b-a^3+ab^2),\qquad U_Y=\operatorname {Spec}B.
\]

Use subscripts `1,2` for the first and second factors. The four diagonal
ideals have the following exact two-generator presentations:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 U_Z\mathbin\times U_Z &(x_1-x_2,\ y_1-y_2),\\
 U_Z\mathbin\times U_Y &(a_2-b_2x_1,\ 1-b_2y_1),\\
 U_Y\mathbin\times U_Z &(a_1-b_1x_2,\ 1-b_1y_2),\\
 U_Y\mathbin\times U_Y &(a_1-a_2,\ b_1-b_2).
\end{array}
\tag{269.1}
\]

For the mixed pairs these equations are just the exact overlap substitutions

\[
 a=x/y,
 \qquad b=1/y,
 \qquad x=a/b,
 \qquad y=1/b.
\tag{269.2}
\]

The second equation in either mixed row forces `b` and `y` to be units on the
closed subscheme, so (269.1) has no extraneous component. Equivalently, its
quotient is the coordinate ring of `U_Z intersection U_Y`. The equal-chart
rows are the kernels of the corresponding multiplication maps. These
presentations verify the ideals exactly, but they do not make them principal.

For `U_Z x U_Z`, suppose that `I_Delta=(g)`. Use the `K`-rational point
`Q=(0,0)` of `U_Z` and specialize the second factor at `Q`. The image of the
diagonal ideal is

\[
 (x-x(Q),y-y(Q))=(x,y)=\mathfrak m_Q,
\]

so the assumption would make `m_Q` principal in `A`.

It is not principal. If `m_Q=(f)`, the regular function `f` on
`E minus {O}` would have precisely one simple zero, at `Q`, and no other zero
on that affine curve. On the projective curve its divisor would consequently
be

\[
 (f)=Q-O.
\]

But under `Pic^0(E) congruent E`, the class of `Q-O` is the nonidentity point
`Q=(0,0)` (indeed a nonzero two-torsion point), and hence is not principal.
This contradiction proves that `I_Delta` is invertible but nonprincipal on
`U_Z x U_Z`.

One exact point handles the other three pairs:

\[
 P=(i,1-i)\in E(K).
\tag{269.3}
\]

It belongs to both opens. Its tangent slope on `y^2=x^3-x` is `-(1+i)`, and
the duplication formula gives

\[
 2P=(0,0)=Q.
\tag{269.4}
\]

Since `1-i` is nonzero, `P` is not two-torsion; hence it has exact order four.
The maximal ideal of `P` in `A` is nonprincipal: a generator would give a
function with divisor `P-O`, contrary to `P ne O` in `Pic^0(E)=E`.

The maximal ideal of `P` in `B` is nonprincipal as well. Indeed the complement
of `U_Y` is the set of the three nonzero two-torsion points

\[
 S=\{(0,0),(1,0),(-1,0)\}.
\]

If that maximal ideal were generated by `f`, then on `E`

\[
 (f)=P+\sum_{T\in S}n_TT
\tag{269.5}
\]

for integers `n_T`. The degree and Abel conditions for a principal divisor
would in particular give `P+sum n_T T=O` in the elliptic-curve group. The sum
of the `T` terms is two-torsion, whereas `P` has order four, a contradiction.

Now specialize the `U_Y` factor of `U_Z x U_Y` at `P`. The image of its
diagonal ideal is the nonprincipal maximal ideal of `P` in `A`. Specializing
the `U_Z` factor of `U_Y x U_Z` at `P` gives the nonprincipal maximal ideal in
`B`. Finally, specializing one factor of `U_Y x U_Y` at `P` gives the same
maximal ideal in `B`. Because an image of a principal ideal is principal, all
three diagonal ideals are nonprincipal. Thus every row of (269.1) is an exact
nonprincipality certificate over `Q(i)`.

There are consequently no chart-pair generators and no transition units to
print on this cover. Transition units exist only after a further open
refinement that trivializes `O(-Delta)`; such a refinement is outside the
frozen model.

## Three-factor tensor test

Index a product chart of `E^6=(E x E)^3` by

\[
 ((c_1,d_1),(c_2,d_2),(c_3,d_3))\in\{Z,Y\}^{2\mathbin\times3}.
\]

All `4^3=64` charts meet the big diagonal, since every intersection
`U_(c_j) intersection U_(d_j)` is nonempty. For `j=1,2,3`, the `j`-th declared
Cartier ideal is the scalar extension of the corresponding row of (269.1).
If this extended ideal were principal, specialize every unused `U_Z` factor
at `Q=(0,0)` and every unused `U_Y` factor at `O=[0:1:0]` (whose `U_Y`
coordinates are `(a,b)=(0,0)`). This retracts the asserted generator to a
generator on the original chart pair, contradicting the census above.

Hence on every one of the 64 product charts all three separately prescribed
factor-diagonal ideals are nonprincipal. In particular there is no scalar
triple `(g_1,g_2,g_3)` and therefore no transition-unit system or chartwise
scalar Koszul complex to tensor.

This is not a failure of geometric regularity. Each factor diagonal is an
effective Cartier divisor, and their intersection is the smooth big diagonal,
a regular immersion of codimension three. After local trivialization, any
three local equations form a regular sequence. On the frozen charts the
requested global scalar sequence does not exist, so it is neither valid nor
meaningful to label it a nonregular sequence. The detected defect is
nonprincipality, strictly before the regular-sequence test.

## Frozen-stop disposition

This is exactly the authorized wall "a chart pair on which no declared
principal diagonal generator is valid." A refinement that trivializes
`O(-Delta)`, a Koszul complex built from nontrivial line bundles, or a different
cover could repair the local presentation, but each changes the frozen direct
Cech--Koszul model and is forbidden in this cycle.

Accordingly the audit stops before chain representatives for `h_i`, `n_i`, or
`alpha`, and before the nine PEL deformation and variation-of-arrow cochains.
No assertion about their cancellation, survival, or rank is made.
