# Cycle 236: `CA235` continuum-admission audit

## Terminal verdict

`CA235 FAIL`.

The Cycle 235 full-Hilbert theorem is retained exactly as a finite-lattice
strong-coupling result. It cannot be used along cutoff removal: its certified
interval has `lambda` extremely small, while four-dimensional asymptotic
freedom requires `lambda(a)=2/g(a)^4 -> infinity`. A search of the surviving
Yang--Mills routes finds no single continuum lemma that simultaneously has
scale-uniform constants, is weaker than the desired continuum mass-gap
assertion, has an exact finite falsifier with an analytic complement bound, and
has a written transfer to an official quantifier.

This is the terminal status required by the Cycle 235 stop rule. It is not a
claim that no suitable continuum lemma exists; it records that none is present
in the current portfolio and forbids retaining Yang--Mills because of the
bounded theorem alone.

## 1. Physical scaling used in the audit

Take isotropic hypercubic lattices with

\[
 a_k=2^{-k}a_0\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad g_k\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \lambda_k={2\over g_k^4}\longrightarrow\infty.       \tag{236.1}
\]

For the normalization `K_lambda=C+lambda W`, the physical Hamiltonian is
`H_(a,g)=g^2 K_(2/g^4)/(2a)`. Thus a positive physical mass `m` requires
`Delta(K_(lambda_k))>=2m a_k/g_k^2`, not merely a positive bound on a fixed
strong-coupling interval.
The admissible observable algebra is the gauge-invariant positive-time algebra
generated by bounded cylindrical Wilson observables, together with any local
field composites whose renormalization and reflection compatibility are
separately proved. Spatial slice-local smearing may be used because it pulls
this algebra into itself; isotropic positive-flow-time smearing may not be
assumed reflection positive.

This specification passes only test 1 of `CA235`. It does not produce an
estimate on the trajectory (236.1).

### 2.1 Bounded strong-coupling theorem

Cycle 235 proves, on its declared periodic tori,

\[
 |\lambda|\le {1\over8(15970360332)^{416}},\qquad
 \Delta_\Lambda\ge {\log3-1\over56\log15970360332}.         \tag{236.2}
\]

Its constants are volume uniform and finitely audited, but there is no overlap
between (236.2) and `lambda_k -> infinity`. Rescaling the energy does not move
the coupling into the certified interval. Candidate 1 therefore fails physical
scaling and official transfer. This is exactly why `BR235 PASS` does not imply
`CA235 PASS`.

### 2.2 Full-complement transfer contraction

For fixed physical `r_0>0`, the tempting inequality is

\[
 \langle f,T_{a_k}^{\lceil r_0/a_k\rceil}f\rangle
 \le q\|f\|^2,\qquad 0<q<1,                                \tag{236.3}
\]

for every physical vacuum-orthogonal `f`, uniformly in `k` and volume. The
spectral theorem would give physical mass at least
`log(1/q)/(a_k ceil(r_0/a_k))`, tending to `log(1/q)/r_0`.

That implication is also the reason (236.3) fails test 2: it assumes the
uniform lattice gap on the same continuum trajectory in semigroup language.
It has no admissible finite falsifier either. The exact escaping-state family
`T_k=qI+(1-q)P_(e_k)` passes every fixed finite trial-space test eventually but
has norm one on the moving complement. A finite Ritz calculation can certify
failure when it finds an eigenvalue above `q`, but cannot certify success
without the very analytic complement estimate being sought.

### 2.3 One-step reflection-positive RG contraction

A genuinely separated candidate would put the exact blocked action in a
Banach space of all loops, polymers, and intertwiners and prove, with explicit
`rho<1` and `C<infinity`, an invariant-domain inequality such as

\[
 \|R A-A_*\|_{\mathcal B}\le\rho\|A-A_*\|_{\mathcal B},
 \qquad \|A\|_{\mathcal B}\le C,                            \tag{236.4}
\]

uniformly at every step on (236.1), with blocking preserving the positive-time
algebra. Iteration would advance cutoff-uniform control at each fixed physical
scale without assuming continuum existence or a spectral gap. Later gates
would still be tightness of all Schwinger functions, nontriviality, restoration
of Euclidean symmetry, OS reconstruction, observable completeness, and a
positive physical mass bound.

The natural finite test would truncate (236.4) to one declared block and
representations `j<=J`, compute the finite coefficients exactly, and enclose the
complement by a formula `Tail(J)<=epsilon_J` strong enough to keep the same
`rho<1`. Success would be the exact finite inequalities plus the analytic tail
bound; failure would be either a finite coefficient violating the invariant
cone/contraction or a proved lower tail bound exceeding the budget.

No such `Tail(J)` estimate is available. Exact straight-link blocking produces
an infinite induced action, and the existing finite common-cone and character
calculations do not control that tail. Omitting it, estimating only finitely
many couplings, or using floating decay would be Ritz evidence without an
analytic complement bound. Candidate 3 therefore fails test 3 and is not an
admissible `CA235` packet.

### 2.4 Slice-smeared observable increments

Another separated target would be a scale-uniform Cauchy estimate for
renormalized gauge-invariant observables,

\[
 \mathbb E|\Phi_{a}(f)-\Phi_{a/2}(f)|^2
 \le C a^{2\eta}\|f\|_X^2,\qquad C,\eta>0,                 \tag{236.5}
\]

with a nonzero variance window and reflection-compatible renormalization.
Spatial slice-local smearing preserves reflection positivity on its image
algebra, so (236.5) would be weaker than the complete Clay assertion.

The current route does not supply (236.5) for a complete local observable
class. The free transverse electric channel retains a contact peak proportional
to `t^(-3/2)a_tau^(-1)`, while isotropic heat flow fails reflection positivity
already in a two-time scalar Hankel determinant. A spatial-only bound therefore
does not restore Euclidean covariance or construct the required local OS
algebra. Its finite tests can reject particular kernels, but no stated
iteration advances an official existence quantifier. Candidate 4 fails test 4.

## 3. Official-transfer accounting

The Clay target requires, for every compact simple `G`, a nontrivial theory on
`R^4` satisfying suitable axioms and a positive Hamiltonian mass gap. The
audited candidates split into two inadmissible classes:

1. (236.2) and finite truncations are genuinely proved but never enter the
   cutoff-removal trajectory, so they advance no continuum quantifier.
2. (236.3)--(236.5) either rename the desired uniform mass bound or lack the
   analytic tail/local-algebra step needed for finite certification and
   iteration.

Even a proof of (236.4) would leave named gates: tightness and consistency,
nontriviality, Euclidean restoration, OS reconstruction, physical observable
completeness, and a scale-uniform mass estimate. This is a valid prospective
transfer chain, but without a fail-closed finite test it does not satisfy all
four `CA235` tests.

bound for its finite falsifier.
