# Cycle 178: physical Wilson low block and electric-Casimir tail

This note implements the low/tail alternative left by Cycle 174 on a finite
spatial lattice. Peter--Weyl theory gives an exact physical decomposition, and
the electric heat semigroup gives an independent tail estimate at strong
coupling. The estimate is finite-volume and explicit. Its constants expose,
rather than solve, the volume and continuum problem.

## Finite-lattice setting

Let `G` be a compact connected Lie group and let a finite spatial lattice have
edge set `E` and plaquette set `P`. After imposing the Gauss law, write

\[
 {\cal H}_{\rm phys}=L^2(G^E)^{{\cal G}},\qquad
 C=\sum_{e\in E}(-\Delta_e).
\]

Boundary conditions may remove global gauge factors but do not affect the
argument. The Peter--Weyl spin-network basis diagonalizes `C`: a network with
edge labels `pi_e` has electric degree

\[
 c(\boldsymbol\pi)=\sum_e C_2(\pi_e).
\]

Thus `P_D=1_[0,D](C)` is a finite-rank, gauge-invariant projection. Its range
is the finite electric-degree Wilson-network space; for matrix groups it is
equivalently generated by bounded-degree Wilson polynomials, with the necessary
intertwiners at vertices.

Use the dimensionless Kogut--Susskind Hamiltonian

\[
 K_\lambda=C+\lambda W,\qquad
 W=\sum_{p\in P}w_p\ge0,
 \qquad 0\le w_p\le w_*.
\]

For the usual normalization,

\[
 H_{a,L}={g^2\over 2a}K_\lambda,
 \qquad \lambda={2\over g^4},
\]

up to conventional group and anisotropy constants. Let `E_0` and `Omega` be
the ground energy and a normalized ground state of `K_lambda`, assume this
ground state is unique, and put

\[
 Q=I-|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|,
 \qquad S_s=Qe^{-s(K_\lambda-E_0)}Q.
\]

The parameter `s` is dimensionless electric time. No infinite-volume or
continuum assertion is used here.

## Exact physical low/tail decomposition

The electric projector need not commute with `Q`: the interacting vacuum is
not the constant electric vacuum. Consequently `QP_DQ` is generally not a
projection, and writing the physical tail as `Q(I-P_D)Q` is not exact.

Define instead

\[
 {\cal L}_D=Q(P_D{\cal H}_{\rm phys})\subset Q{\cal H}_{\rm phys},
 \qquad \Pi_D=P_{{\cal L}_D}.
\]

Since `P_D` has finite rank, `cal L_D` is closed. For `f in Q cal H`,

\[
 f\perp{\cal L}_D
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \langle f,Qp\rangle=0\quad(p\in P_D{\cal H})
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P_Df=0.
\]

Hence the exact orthogonal decomposition after both the Gauss and vacuum
quotients is

\[
 Q{\cal H}_{\rm phys}
 ={\cal L}_D\ \widehat\oplus\
   \bigl(Q{\cal H}_{\rm phys}\cap\ker P_D\bigr).
\tag{178.1}
\]

This removes a small but important ambiguity in the Cycle 174 proposal. The
low block is the vacuum-subtracted image of finite-degree Wilson networks; the
tail consists exactly of physical, vacuum-orthogonal vectors having no
Peter--Weyl component of electric degree at most `D`.

## Independent electric tail estimate

Let

\[
 \Lambda_D=\inf\bigl(\sigma(C)\cap(D,\infty)\bigr),
 \qquad B_L=\|W\|\le w_*|P|.
\]

For `f in ker P_D`, the electric semigroup obeys
`||e^{-uC}f||<=e^{-u Lambda_D}||f||`. Duhamel's formula, in the ordering that
keeps the electric semigroup next to `f`, gives

\[
 e^{-sK_\lambda}f=e^{-sC}f
 -\lambda\int_0^s e^{-(s-u)K_\lambda}W e^{-uC}f\,du.
\]

Both semigroups are contractions because `C,W>=0`. Therefore

\[
 \|e^{-sK_\lambda}f\|
 \le R_D^{\rm bare}(s)\|f\|,
\]

where

\[
 R_D^{\rm bare}(s)
 =e^{-s\Lambda_D}
  +{\lambda B_L\over\Lambda_D}
    (1-e^{-s\Lambda_D}).
\tag{178.2}
\]

Restoring the ground-energy normalization and applying `Q` can only give

\[
 \|S_s(I-\Pi_D)\|
 \le R_D(s):=e^{sE_0}R_D^{\rm bare}(s).
\tag{178.3}
\]

This is an actual moving-tail operator bound, not density, a diagonal Wilson
correlator, or a statement about each fixed state. It is independent of any
Wilson estimate on the low block. It is also deliberately stated with the
ground-energy factor visible; discarding that factor would incorrectly treat
the interacting electric vacuum as the physical vacuum.

The one-plaquette boundary calculation in
`cycle-178-wilson-low-tail-adversary.md` is consistent with this result. Bare
magnetic multiplication has an order-one cross-cutoff matrix element, while
(178.2) controls the full ordered semigroup only because electric heat acts
next to the tail input.

The elementary estimates

\[
 B_L\le w_*|P|,
 \qquad
 0\le E_0\le\lambda\langle1,W1\rangle
              \le\lambda w_*|P|
\tag{178.4}
\]

make (178.3) completely explicit on every finite lattice.

## Finite-lattice low/tail theorem

**Theorem.** In the preceding setting, suppose a whole-block Wilson estimate,
not merely a generatorwise estimate, proves

\[
 q_D(s):=\|S_s\Pi_D\|<1.
\]

Then

\[
 \|S_s\|\le
 \eta_D(s):=\sqrt{q_D(s)^2+R_D(s)^2}.
\tag{178.5}
\]

In particular, if `eta_D(s)<1`, the finite-lattice dimensionless Hamiltonian
has the gap

\[
 \operatorname{gap}(K_\lambda)
 \ge-{1\over s}\log\eta_D(s)>0,
\tag{178.6}
\]

and

\[
 \operatorname{gap}(H_{a,L})
 \ge {g^2\over2a}
       \left[-{1\over s}\log\eta_D(s)\right].
\tag{178.7}
\]

Indeed, for a unit vector `f` in the vacuum complement, orthogonality in
(178.1) gives `||Pi_D f||^2+||(I-Pi_D)f||^2=1`. Thus

\[
 \|S_sf\|\le q_D\|\Pi_Df\|+R_D\|(I-\Pi_D)f\|
 \le\sqrt{q_D^2+R_D^2}.
\]

The spectral theorem then gives (178.6). This is exactly the composable
low/tail criterion of Cycle 174, now with a canonical physical low projection
and an explicit Casimir-tail term.

There is also an unconditional finite-volume strong-coupling check. Let
`c_*(L)` be the first positive eigenvalue of `C` on the physical space. The
min--max principle and `0<=lambda W<=lambda B_L` imply

\[
 E_1(K_\lambda)\ge c_*(L),
 \qquad E_0(K_\lambda)\le\lambda B_L,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{gap}(K_\lambda)\ge c_*(L)-\lambda B_L.
\tag{178.8}
\]

Thus every fixed finite lattice has a rigorously positive gap whenever
`lambda B_L<c_*(L)`. This is stronger than a two-sided bounded-perturbation
estimate because the magnetic perturbation is positive. It is not
volume-uniform: the elementary bound `B_L<=w_*|P|` shrinks the certified
strong-coupling interval like the inverse volume.

For a family of volumes, (178.5) immediately gives the following exact
uniform-volume criterion. If there are cutoffs `D_L` and constants `q,r` with

\[
 \sup_L q_{D_L,L}(s)\le q,
 \qquad
 \sup_L e^{sE_{0,L}}
 \left[e^{-s\Lambda_{D_L}}+
 {\lambda\|W_L\|\over\Lambda_{D_L}}
 (1-e^{-s\Lambda_{D_L}})\right]\le r,
 \qquad q^2+r^2<1,
\tag{178.9}
\]

then `inf_L gap(K_(lambda,L))>=-s^(-1)log sqrt(q^2+r^2)>0`. This theorem is
uniform in volume, allows the Peter--Weyl cutoff to move with the volume, and
contains no dimension factor from the tail space. The issue is whether its
hypotheses can be proved with a low block that remains controllable.

At `lambda=0`, electric degree is invariant and the two images are orthogonal;
the sharper bound is `max(q_D,e^{-s Lambda_D})`. For `lambda>0`, multiplication
by a plaquette character changes Peter--Weyl labels, so this orthogonality and
tail invariance are lost. Formula (178.2) pays explicitly for that mixing.

## What is and is not uniform in volume

The electric threshold `Lambda_D` and heat factor in (178.2) do not depend on
the number of plaquettes. The perturbation term does:

\[
 {\lambda B_L\over\Lambda_D}
 \le {\lambda w_*|P|\over\Lambda_D},
 \qquad e^{sE_0}\le e^{s\lambda w_*|P|}.
\tag{178.10}
\]

Consequently the direct Casimir/Duhamel proof is volume-uniform only under an
additional estimate that cancels the vacuum energy density and replaces the
extensive operator norm of `W` by a local connected bound. With (178.4) alone,
one must take roughly `Lambda_D` at least of order `lambda|P|` (and much larger
if the crude exponential is retained). The low Wilson block then grows with
the volume, so no regulator-independent complexity scale has been obtained.

This failure is structural, not just a poor choice of notation. Even in a
product rotor model, a perturbed one-site ground state `phi_lambda` gives an
`N`-fold tensor-product ground state. If its constant-mode probability
is less than one, the probability that the total Peter--Weyl degree is at most
any fixed `D` tends to zero as `N` tends to infinity. A fixed total-degree
cutoff therefore places almost all of the dressed vacuum in its "tail." Vacuum
subtraction repairs the exact Hilbert decomposition (178.1), but it does not
supply the connected estimates needed to remove this extensive dressing from
(178.3).

A genuine uniform-volume strong-coupling theorem must add something of the
following strength:

1. a linked-cluster or polymer ground-state transform that cancels `E_0` per
   unit volume;
2. a local degree/cluster tail whose boundary norm is controlled independently
   of `|P|`;
3. a uniform estimate on the full low block, including coherent combinations
   of Wilson networks; and
4. stability under boundary conditions and removal of all exactly transmitted
   sectors.

Those inputs are substantially stronger than Peter--Weyl diagonalization.
They are the missing uniform-volume lemma, not consequences of (178.2).

## Continuum obstruction

There are two separate limits, and the finite theorem settles neither.

First, four-dimensional continuum scaling approaches the ultraviolet fixed
point with bare `g(a)->0` by asymptotic freedom. In the normalization above the
strong-coupling expansion parameter is `lambda=2/g(a)^4`, which becomes large,
not small. A cluster estimate valid only for `lambda<lambda_*` therefore does
not cover the continuum trajectory. One would need a reflection-positive RG
construction carrying the weak-coupling lattice measure to a fixed physical
scale inside a proven strong-coupling basin, uniformly in volume, while
retaining the full induced loop/polymer action. Cycle 83 explains why a finite
coupling truncation does not establish this entry.

Second, even a volume-uniform bound `eta<1` at fixed lattice spacing would prove
only a lattice Hamiltonian gap. To obtain the Clay conclusion one must also
construct a nontrivial OS limit, identify the vacuum complements, pass the
fixed-physical-time contraction without escaping spectral states, restore
Euclidean symmetry, and verify the required axioms and observable
completeness.

Thus Cycle 178 produces an exact finite-lattice theorem and the requested
independent electric tail mechanism. The main candidate reduces to a precise
new target: replace the extensive right side of (178.3) by a connected,
vacuum-renormalized tail contraction uniform in volume, and then prove RG basin
entry from the asymptotically free continuum trajectory. Neither target is
proved here, so no Yang--Mills construction or mass gap is claimed.
