# Cycle 233: Yarotsky's boundary-polymer and full-Hilbert gap step

## Verdict

The bulk activity estimate and a Kotecky--Preiss (KP) inequality do not, by
themselves, make Yarotsky's numerical spectral gap explicit.  They do make the
gap in the cyclic subspace generated by the product vacuum explicit once one
uses a tilted KP estimate.  If

\[
 \#\{\chi:x\in\operatorname{supp}\chi,
              |\operatorname{supp}\chi|=n\}\le c^n,
 \qquad |w(\chi)|\le\eta^{|\operatorname{supp}\chi|},             \tag{233.1}
\]

then every

\[
 0<\theta<\theta_*:=\log\frac1{2ec\eta}                         \tag{233.2}
\]

is an admissible exponential tilt, provided `2 e c eta < 1`.  Conditional on
the standard pinned-cluster form of KP, this gives a normalized cyclic-subspace
gap at least `theta_*/t_0` (the endpoint follows by taking
`theta` upward to `theta_*`).

For the repaired hostile bulk count in Cycle 232,

\[
 c'=2\,191^4=2\,661\,726\,722,
 \quad q'=6c'=15\,970\,360\,332,
 \quad \eta'=(q')^{-1},                                      \tag{233.3}
\]

one has `c' eta'=1/6`, and therefore

\[
 \theta_*=\log(3/e)=\log3-1.                                 \tag{233.4}
\]

The exact-support choice `t_0=14 log q'` therefore gives the explicit
bulk/cyclic bounds

\[
 \Delta_{\rm cyc}(4K_\lambda)\ge
 {\log 3-1\over14\log15970360332},
 \qquad
 \Delta_{\rm cyc}(K_\lambda)\ge
 {\log 3-1\over56\log15970360332}.                            \tag{233.5}
\]

The physical number in (233.5) is approximately `7.495 x 10^-5`.  It applies
at the Cycle 232 bulk coupling certificate

\[
 |\lambda|\le {1\over8(15970360332)^{416}}.                   \tag{233.6}
\]

These are not yet published-proof full-Hilbert gap bounds.  The later part of
Yarotsky's proof does not define the polymers produced by replacing the product
vacuum by an arbitrary vector, does not prove their activity or counting
estimate, and does not state a uniform tilted cluster estimate for them.  It
only says that the estimate remains valid "for `v` small enough."  Consequently
an exact full-Hilbert lower bound cannot be extracted from the published
details.  Equations (233.4)--(233.6) are rigorous outputs of the repaired bulk
polymer
calculation for the product-vacuum cyclic subspace, and become full-Hilbert
bounds only after the boundary-insertion estimate isolated below is proved.

## 1. What tilted KP supplies exactly

Let `s(chi)=|supp chi|` and give a polymer the KP weight

\[
 a(\chi)=s(\chi),\qquad d(\chi)=\theta s(\chi).
\]

For a polymer `chi`, (233.1) gives

\[
\begin{split}
 \sum_{\chi'\not\sim\chi}|w(\chi')|
 e^{a(\chi')+d(\chi')}
 &\le s(\chi)\sum_{n\ge1}
       (c\eta e^{1+\theta})^n\\
 &=s(\chi){x_\theta\over1-x_\theta},
 \qquad x_\theta:=c\eta e^{1+\theta}.                         \tag{233.7}
\end{split}
\]

Hence the usual KP hypothesis with right side `a(chi)` holds whenever
`x_theta <= 1/2`.  Taking strict inequality avoids any issue over which
endpoint convention a cited version of KP uses and yields exactly (233.2).

The form of the cluster theorem needed after (233.7) is not merely convergence
of `log Z`.  If `W(X)` is the Ursell weight of a cluster and

\[
  \|X\|_s:=\sum_{\chi\in X}n_X(\chi)s(\chi),
\]

one needs the pinned estimate

\[
 \sum_{X:\,\operatorname{supp}X\cap S\ne\varnothing}
 |W(X)|e^{\theta\|X\|_s}\le C_{\rm KP}|S|                    \tag{233.8}
\]

for each finite set of space-time points `S`, with a finite convention constant
from the chosen KP theorem.  Formula (233.8) follows pointwise from the standard
pinned KP cluster bound.  For each `x in S`, adjoin a formal singleton pin at
`x`, differentiate the cluster expansion in its activity at zero, and then sum
over `x`.  The KP check for each pin is

\[
 \sum_{\chi:\,x\in\operatorname{supp}\chi}
 |w(\chi)|e^{(1+\theta)s(\chi)}
 \le {x_\theta\over1-x_\theta}\le1.
\]

Thus a convention in which the pinned theorem carries an `e^{a}` factor still
gives an absolute constant (for example `e` when the singleton pin has
`a=1`), followed by the union bound over `x in S`.  One must not instead use a
single pin with `a=|S|` and then call its possible `e^{|S|}` factor an absolute
constant.  This pointwise construction proves (233.8) with a constant
independent of `S`, `N`, and `Lambda`.

For Yarotsky's connected space-time supports, a cluster of time length `l(X)`
satisfies

\[
 l(X)\le\|X\|_s.                                               \tag{233.9}
\]

Indeed, a chain of intersecting supports spanning `l` time steps has at least
one support point on every intervening layer.  Repeated copies of a polymer do
not invalidate this inequality: `||X||_s` charges every copy with its
multiplicity, whereas `l(X)` only sees the union's time span.  Combining
(233.8)--(233.9)
controls clusters longer than `N`.  The factor `l(X)-N` in Yarotsky's remainder
costs no exponential rate: for `0<delta<theta`,

\[
 (l-N)_+e^{-\theta l}
 \le M_{\theta-\delta}e^{-\delta N},\qquad
 M_a:=\sup_{m\ge1}m e^{-am}<\infty.                           \tag{233.10}
\]

After pinning the initial layer and summing its spatial points, (233.8) and
(233.10) give

\[
 \log Z_{N,\Lambda}=a_{1,\Lambda}+a_{2,\Lambda}N
       +O_{\Lambda,\delta}(e^{-\delta N})                    \tag{233.11}
\]

for every `delta<theta`.  The volume dependence of the prefactor is irrelevant
to the finite-volume spectral gap; the exponent is volume independent.

Since one Euclidean step is `t_0`, (233.11) excludes cyclic-subspace spectrum
in `(E_Lambda,E_Lambda+delta/t_0)`.  Letting first `delta` approach `theta`
and then `theta` approach `theta_*` proves the cyclic lower bound
`theta_*/t_0`.  This is the precise replacement for Yarotsky's informal
identification `a_3=-log epsilon`: the activity parameter itself is not the
cluster-tail parameter after entropy has been summed.

Here `E_Lambda` in the bulk-only statement means the bottom of the spectral
measure of `H_Lambda` seen by `Omega_0`, equivalently the bottom of the
restriction to its cyclic subspace.  The inference uses positivity, not a
formal comparison of logarithms.  Writing
`E_Lambda=-a_(2,Lambda)/t_0` and `A_Lambda=exp(a_(1,Lambda))`, (233.11) gives

\[
 \langle e^{-t_0NH_\Lambda}\Omega_0,\Omega_0\rangle
 =A_\Lambda e^{-t_0NE_\Lambda}
   (1+O_{\Lambda,\delta}(e^{-\delta N})),\qquad A_\Lambda>0.
\]

The spectral theorem then forces an atom of mass `A_Lambda` at `E_Lambda` and
no spectral-measure support in
`(E_Lambda,E_Lambda+delta/t_0)`.  Integer Euclidean times suffice: a positive
mass in that interval would decay more slowly than the displayed remainder.
This argument says nothing about spectral subspaces orthogonal to `Omega_0`;
identifying `E_Lambda` with the bottom of the full spectrum requires the
boundary-insertion/completeness step below.

For comparison only, the superseded Cycle 231 candidate count had
`c=72962`, `q=12c=875544`, and `c eta=1/12`, which would give

\[
 \theta_*=\log(6/e)=\log6-1.                                 \tag{233.12}
\]

Its exact-geometry choice `t_0=14 log q` would conditionally give

\[
 \Delta_{\rm cyc}(K_\lambda)
 \ge {\log6-1\over56\log875544}.                             \tag{233.13}
\]

That number is not certified because the old count is false; it is retained
only to distinguish the KP arithmetic from the repaired combinatorial input.

## 2. What the published boundary argument actually says

After deriving the bulk expansion, Yarotsky first obtains a gap only "in the
cyclic subspace generated by" the product vacuum.  To pass to the whole Hilbert
space, the paper replaces `Omega_{Lambda,0}` by `Omega_{Lambda,0}+v` in

\[
 Z_{N,\Lambda}(u)=\langle e^{-t_0NH_\Lambda}u,u\rangle.       \tag{233.14}
\]

It then says:

1. the three terms involving `v` add new polymers `chi_v`;
2. `supp chi_v` contains the entire initial layer, the entire final layer, or
   both;
3. for `v` small enough, `|w(chi)|<=epsilon^{|supp chi|}` remains valid;
4. clusters containing a new polymer touch a time boundary and contribute
   `c+O(epsilon^N)`.

No definition of `chi_v`, no formula for its activity, no count of such
polymers, and no estimate behind steps 3--4 is given.  This omission is
quantitatively serious.  A polymer containing `{0} x Lambda` has support at
least `|Lambda|`; even the bare desired estimate requires a radius resembling

\[
 \|v\|\lesssim C_\Lambda^{-1}\eta^{|\Lambda|},              \tag{233.15}
\]

where the unpublished `C_Lambda` must absorb the decomposition and boundary
configuration count.  A volume-dependent radius is not itself fatal: for each
fixed volume, an arbitrarily small open ball is enough to test every Hilbert
space direction by rescaling.  The fatal point for numerical extraction is
that the paper never proves that one radius works for every `N` with the same
tilted exponent, nor supplies `C_Lambda` or the marked-polymer sum from which
that fact would follow.

The sentence that a boundary cluster contributes `c+O(epsilon^N)` also hides a
necessary separation.  Clusters touching only the initial or only the final
time face alter the constant term.  Only clusters connecting the two faces
are exponentially small.  Establishing this requires a tilted, pinned estimate
for clusters containing the marked boundary polymer; ordinary unmarked KP
convergence does not state it.

## 3. A sufficient boundary-polymer lemma

The missing statement can be isolated without committing to a particular
packaging of the boundary configurations.  For each periodic finite volume
`Lambda`, it is enough to prove the following at every
`0<theta<theta_*`.

**Boundary insertion lemma.** There is `r_Lambda>0` such that, for every
`v` with `||v||<r_Lambda` and every `N`, the logarithm of (233.14), with
`u=Omega_{Lambda,0}+v`, has a convergent marked-polymer expansion satisfying

\[
 \log Z_{N,\Lambda}(\Omega_0+v)
 =b_{0,\Lambda}(v)+b_{1,\Lambda}N+R_{N,\Lambda}(v),           \tag{233.16}
\]

where `b_{1,Lambda}` is the same as for `v=0` and, for every
`0<delta<theta`,

\[
 |R_{N,\Lambda}(v)|
 \le C_{\Lambda,v,\delta}e^{-\delta N}.                      \tag{233.17}
\]

A directly checkable polymer-level version is:

\[
 \sum_{X:\,X\text{ contains a marked boundary polymer and spans }N}
 |W_v(X)|e^{\theta\|X\|_s}\le C_{\Lambda,v},                \tag{233.18}
\]

together with absolute summability of clusters touching only one face.  It is
also sufficient to prove a marked activity estimate and counting estimate that
allow the marked polymers to be inserted into (233.7)--(233.8).  In that
formulation one must state explicitly:

1. how all boundary-connected configuration components are packaged into one
   marked polymer;
2. a bound on the sum of their absolute activities, not just each formal term;
3. an `N`-independent radius `r_Lambda`;
4. the same tilt `theta` as in the bulk KP estimate;
5. separate one-face and two-face cluster bounds.

The constants `r_Lambda` and `C_Lambda,v,delta` may depend on the finite
spatial volume.  Only `theta`, and hence the spectral-gap exponent, must be
uniform in `Lambda`.

## 4. Why this lemma gives the full-Hilbert gap

Let `P_I` be the spectral projection of `H_Lambda` onto an interval

\[
 I=(E_\Lambda,E_\Lambda+\delta/t_0).
\]

The bulk expansion gives `P_I Omega_0=0`.  If (233.16)--(233.17) holds on an
open ball around `Omega_0`, the spectral theorem applied to the Laplace
transform (233.14) gives

\[
 P_I(\Omega_0+v)=0,\qquad \|v\|<r_\Lambda.                    \tag{233.19}
\]

For arbitrary `psi in H_Lambda`, choose nonzero `s` with
`||s psi||<r_Lambda`.  Equation (233.19) and `P_I Omega_0=0` imply
`s P_I psi=0`.  Thus `P_I=0` on the complete Hilbert space.  Repeating this
for all `delta<theta<theta_*` gives

\[
 H_\Lambda\big|_{\Omega_\Lambda^\perp}
 \ge E_\Lambda+{\theta_*\over t_0},                           \tag{233.20}
\]

once ground-state uniqueness is known.

For bounded Wilson perturbations, uniqueness can then be transported from
zero coupling by norm-resolvent continuity in each finite volume: the same
polymer bounds apply along the segment from `0` to `lambda`, (233.20) prevents
the isolated ground projection from meeting the rest of the spectrum, and its
rank remains one.  For the paper's general relatively form-bounded setting,
one must instead spell out the corresponding type-(B) form-resolvent
continuity.  Yarotsky only refers to a continuity argument and prints no
constant, but continuity does not reduce the gap exponent if (233.20) has
already been established uniformly along the segment.

## 5. Audit consequences

1. **Bulk/cyclic result.** Under the repaired Cycle 232 activity and count, the tilted
   KP calculation gives the exact cyclic exponents (233.4)--(233.5), not merely
   existence of some positive exponent.
2. **Full-Hilbert result.** The same numbers are conditional on the boundary
   insertion lemma.  The published proof does not contain enough information
   to assign its constants or verify (233.18).
3. **Cycle 231 wording.** Its old numerical count and endpoint are retracted;
   the comparison in (233.12)--(233.13) is conditional only.
4. **Cycle 232 boundary.** Its repaired rational endpoint is an explicit bulk
   activity/KP certificate, not a full-Hilbert gap theorem; the marked
   expansion still has to be constructed.
5. **Spatial boundaries.** Everything above concerns temporal boundary
   insertions in periodic spatial volumes.  Arbitrary open spatial boxes need
   a separate definition of boundary Hamiltonian terms and a new uniform
   geometry/count audit.
6. **Gauge restriction.** Once an ambient full-Hilbert gap is proved, the
   commuting Gauss projection does not reduce it.  It cannot repair the
   missing ambient boundary-insertion step.

## 6. Hostile re-audit of the cyclic claim

The four possible failure points in the bulk-to-cyclic inference survive the
hostile check, subject to the hypotheses already printed in (233.1):

1. `l(X)<=||X||_s` is valid because cluster connectivity supplies an
   intersecting chain across every intermediate time layer, and the right side
   counts polymer support with multiplicity.
2. The required pinned estimate follows from singleton pins and a union bound.
   A single `S`-sized ghost pin would not justify an `S`-independent convention
   constant, so that shortcut is retracted.
3. Tilted absolute summability gives both finite coefficients in (233.11) and
   the `O(e^{-delta N})` remainder for every `delta<theta`; the polynomial
   factor `l(X)-N` does not consume the endpoint after taking suprema as in
   (233.10).
4. Positivity of the spectral measure converts (233.11) into a gap for exactly
   the cyclic restriction.  It does not convert it into a full-Hilbert gap.

Accordingly, the conditional bulk/cyclic number `theta_*/t_0` is retained.
Any wording that promotes it to a full-Hilbert gap without (233.16)--(233.18)
remains retracted.
