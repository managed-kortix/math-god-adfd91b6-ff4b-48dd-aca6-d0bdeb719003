# Unit-cell endpoint bounds for the complete endpoint functional

## Scope

This note stays in the direct sawtooth representation. It gives elementary
cellwise lower and upper bounds for the complete `N -> 2N` functional, an
`O(T log N)` finite certificate based on divisor impulses, and two obstructions
to a purely local positivity proof. No Fourier expansion, dense frequency
kernel, or common period is used.

Write

\[
 f(t)=1+\sum_{a\le 2N}u_a\{t/a\},\qquad
 d(t)=\sum_{a\le 2N}d_a\{t/a\},\qquad \alpha>0,
\]

where in the endpoint application `f=F_N`, `d=F_N-H_N`, and

\[
 F_{2N}=f-\alpha d.
\]

The complete decrement density is

\[
 q(t)=2f(t)d(t)-\alpha d(t)^2
     ={f(t)^2-F_{2N}(t)^2\over\alpha}.
\]

Consequently

\[
 P_N-P_{2N}=\alpha\int_1^\infty q(t){dt\over t^2}.
\]

All assertions below apply to arbitrary real coefficient vectors `u,d`; the
Möbius structure enters only through their values.

## 1. Exact cell reduction

On the open unit cell `I_k=(k,k+1)`, put

\[
 A=\sum_a{u_a\over a},\quad D=\sum_a{d_a\over a},
\]

\[
 b_k=1-\sum_a u_a\lfloor k/a\rfloor,\qquad
 e_k=-\sum_a d_a\lfloor k/a\rfloor.
\]

Then

\[
 f(t)=At+b_k,\qquad d(t)=Dt+e_k,
\]

and, with `ell=2f-alpha d`,

\[
 q(t)=d(t)\ell(t)=c_2t^2+c_{1,k}t+c_{0,k},
\]

where

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 c_2&=2AD-\alpha D^2,\\
 c_{1,k}&=2(Ae_k+b_kD)-2\alpha De_k,\\
 c_{0,k}&=2b_ke_k-\alpha e_k^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Thus the exact cell functional is

\[
 \boxed{J_k:=\int_k^{k+1}q(t){dt\over t^2}
 =c_2+c_{1,k}\log(1+1/k)+{c_{0,k}\over k(k+1)}.}
\]

This is already a complete-functional certificate: only arithmetic, one
logarithm per cell, and outward rounding are needed.

## 2. Endpoint-only lower and upper bounds

Set `s=t-k` and use the one-sided affine endpoint values

\[
 d_0=d(k+),\quad d_1=d((k+1)-),\qquad
 \ell_0=\ell(k+),\quad \ell_1=\ell((k+1)-).
\]

Define the three Bernstein products

\[
 Q_0=d_0\ell_0,\qquad
 Q_{01}={d_0\ell_1+d_1\ell_0\over2},\qquad
 Q_1=d_1\ell_1.
\]

Since

\[
 q(k+s)=Q_0(1-s)^2+2Q_{01}s(1-s)+Q_1s^2,
\]

integration against the positive weight `(k+s)^-2` gives positive numbers
`beta_0,beta_01,beta_1` whose sum is `1/[k(k+1)]` and

\[
 J_k=\beta_0Q_0+\beta_{01}Q_{01}+\beta_1Q_1.
\]

Explicitly, with

\[
 m_0={1\over k(k+1)},\quad
 m_1=\log(1+1/k)-{1\over k+1},
\]

\[
 m_2={2k+1\over k+1}-2k\log(1+1/k),
\]

one has

\[
 \beta_0=m_0-2m_1+m_2,\quad
 \beta_{01}=2(m_1-m_2),\quad
 \beta_1=m_2.
\]

Each beta is strictly positive. Therefore the elementary endpoint enclosure is

\[
 \boxed{{\min(Q_0,Q_{01},Q_1)\over k(k+1)}\le J_k\le
 {\max(Q_0,Q_{01},Q_1)\over k(k+1)}.}
\]

In particular, `Q_0,Q_01,Q_1 >= 0` certifies nonnegative contribution of the
whole cell. The mixed endpoint product is indispensable: positivity of only
`q(k+)` and `q((k+1)-)` does not prevent two interlacing affine zeros and a
negative interior interval.

The same formula permits block certificates. One may sum exact `J_k` on
ambiguous cells and use the endpoint lower bound on easy cells. This retains
the complete constant, linear, and oscillatory contributions automatically.

## 3. What the discriminant does and does not prove

The cell discriminant has the special identity

\[
 \boxed{c_{1,k}^2-4c_2c_{0,k}=4(Db_k-e_kA)^2\ge0.}
\]

The cancellation of `alpha` is exact. It follows simply because `q=d ell` is
a product of two affine functions.

This identity is useful for locating the bad interval: if `c_2>0`, the density
is negative precisely between its two real roots; if `c_2<0`, the signs are
reversed. It is not a positivity mechanism. The discriminant is automatically
a square for every coefficient vector, so its nonnegativity contains no
Möbius cancellation. A discriminant-based proof must additionally control the
root order or the weighted mass between the roots; that information is
equivalent to controlling the endpoint factors above.

## 4. Cumulative floor sums and a sparse certificate

The floor sums evolve by divisor impulses:

\[
 \boxed{b_{k+1}=b_k-\sum_{a\mid k+1}u_a,\qquad
 e_{k+1}=e_k-\sum_{a\mid k+1}d_a,}
\]

with `a<=2N` understood. Equivalently,

\[
 \sum_a u_a\lfloor k/a\rfloor
 =\sum_{n\le k}\sum_{a\mid n}u_a,
\]

and similarly for `d`. Hence all cells through `T` can be generated by a
truncated divisor sieve in

\[
 O\!\left(\sum_{a\le2N}T/a\right)=O(T\log N)
\]

arithmetic operations and `O(T+N)` storage, or `O(N)` working storage if the
impulses are streamed. There is no quadratic pair kernel. Once `b_k,e_k` are
known, each exact cell integral or endpoint enclosure costs `O(1)`.

This is the natural elementary scalable certificate:

1. form the endpoint coefficient channels `u,d` with outward-rounded logs;
2. precompute `A,D`;
3. sieve the two divisor-impulse sequences;
4. update `b_k,e_k` and sum exact cell intervals, using endpoint bounds where
   they are already decisive;
5. enclose the uncomputed tail pointwise.

For the last step put

\[
 F_*=1+\sum_a|u_a|,\qquad D_*=\sum_a|d_a|,
\quad C_*=2F_*D_*+\alpha D_*^2.
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{\left|\int_T^\infty q(t){dt\over t^2}\right|\le {C_*\over T}.}
\]

If `S_T=sum_{1<=k<T} J_k`, the interval

\[
 \boxed{S_T-C_*/T\le\int_1^\infty q(t){dt\over t^2}
 \le S_T+C_*/T}
\]

is a complete certificate. It proves decrement when the lower endpoint is
positive and proves an increment when the upper endpoint is negative. The
bound is polynomial-cost because the coefficient `l1` masses are finite and
explicit, although it may still require a large `T`.

## 5. Local positivity obstruction

Cellwise positivity is false already at small scale. A 192-bit Arb evaluation
of the exact direct cell formula gives, for the `N=8 -> 16` endpoint channels,

\[
 J_{35}=-0.0001179347729698330851071516181067994\ldots<0.
\]

For `N=16 -> 32`, negative cells occur earlier, including `k=26,27,35,38`.
Thus no proof can require every unit cell to contribute positively. The viable
elementary forms are instead:

- a prefix or block lower bound in which positive cells absorb negative ones;
- a pairing rule stated in terms of divisor impulses and endpoint products;
- a cumulative-floor-sum inequality directly controlling `sum J_k`.

There is a second limitation. The floor-sum recurrence is sparse, but its
quadratic term `2b_ke_k-alpha e_k^2` retains the signed correlation between the
two Möbius divisor transforms. Abel summation can move the weights
`log(1+1/k)` and `1/[k(k+1)]` onto cumulative sums, but it does not remove this
correlation. Accordingly, the sieve is a scalable finite certificate, not by
itself an asymptotic decrement theorem.

## Verdict

Endpoint data do control each complete unit-cell functional: three Bernstein
products give rigorous lower and upper bounds, and the exact integral uses one
logarithm. Cumulative floor sums turn the full finite calculation into a sparse
`O(T log N)` divisor-sieve certificate independent of dense frequency kernels.

The discriminant supplies only root geometry, not positivity, because it is
identically a square. Moreover, certified negative cells rule out an
all-cells-positive strategy. The remaining scalable analytic target is a block
inequality for the cumulative divisor impulses that dominates the explicitly
identified negative cells while preserving the signed quadratic correlation.
