# Cycle 42: correlation-preserving lower bounds for completed-square packets

## 1. Block Hilbert-space formulation

Retain the Cycle 41 notation

\[
 d_{n,k}=(\ell_n+\lambda_kv_{n,k},\sqrt{q_k}v_{n,k}),\qquad
 u_{n,k}=(m_n+\lambda_ku_{n,k},\sqrt{q_k}u_{n,k}),
\]

so that `mathcal D_(n,k)=|d_(n,k)|^2` and
`mathcal U_(n,k)=|u_(n,k)|^2`. For a finite block `[A,B)` and a cell cutoff
`K`, form vectors in the direct sum of the two-dimensional cell spaces:

\[
 X=(\sqrt{\beta_n}d_{n,k})_{A\le n<B,\ k\le K},\qquad
 Y=(\sqrt{w_n}u_{n,k})_{A\le n<B,\ k\le K}.             \tag{42.1}
\]

Put

\[
 P=\|X\|^2,\qquad N=\|Y\|^2,\qquad J=\langle X,Y\rangle. \tag{42.2}
\]

Then the truncated block residual is exactly `P-N`. Since
`w_n=beta_n C_n`, the mixed contraction is

\[
 \boxed{J=\sum_{A\le n<B}\beta_n\sqrt{C_n}
 \sum_{k\le K}\left[(\ell_n+\lambda_kv_{n,k})
 (m_n+\lambda_ku_{n,k})+q_kv_{n,k}u_{n,k}\right].}       \tag{42.3}
\]

Substitution of the finite divisor formulas (41.7)--(41.10) leaves every
Mobius sign inside the bilinear contraction. In particular, admission of a
new divisor changes each linear coordinate before (42.3) is formed. Thus the
old--new terms are retained, unlike in separate upper bounds for `P` and `N`.
The limit `K to infinity` recovers the complete block because both norms are
finite.

## 2. The strongest scalar Cauchy bound and its exact defect

Cauchy--Schwarz gives the correlation-preserving lower bound

\[
 \boxed{P-N\ge {|J|^2\over N}-N\qquad(N>0).}            \tag{42.4}
\]

This is useful only when `|J|>=N`. More importantly, orthogonal projection
gives the exact identity

\[
 \boxed{P-N={|J|^2\over N}-N+
 \left\|X-{J\over N}Y\right\|^2.}                       \tag{42.5}
\]

Therefore (42.4) is not merely a possibly loose estimate: its entire loss is
the D mass orthogonal to the aggregate U packet. Positive recovery may occur
inside precisely this discarded component.

An Arb evaluation using the complete restricted Vasyunin Gram matrix confirms
that this is the obstruction on every Cycle 41 negative-band first-recovery
block. The displayed decimals are midpoints of narrow certified balls.

## 3. Multiprobe projection lemma

There is a direct large-sieve refinement which does not discard the missing
directions. Let `z_1,...,z_r` be any block-cell vectors, let

\[
 G_{ij}=\langle z_i,z_j\rangle,\qquad
 b_i=\langle X,z_i\rangle,
\]

and let `G^+` denote the Moore--Penrose inverse.

**Lemma (correlation-preserving dual lower bound).** One has

\[
 \boxed{P-N\ge b^*G^+b-N.}                              \tag{42.6}
\]

Moreover, `b^*G^+b` is exactly the squared norm of the projection of `X` onto
`span(z_1,...,z_r)`. If a new probe `z` is adjoined and
`z_perp=(I-P_Z)z`, its exact gain is

\[
 \boxed{{|\langle X,z_\perp\rangle|^2\over\|z_\perp\|^2}.} \tag{42.7}
\]

Proof: minimize `||X-Zc||^2` over `c`, or equivalently complete the Gram
quadratic. Formula (42.7) is the one-dimensional orthogonal update.

Take the first probe to be `Y`. For subsequent probes, use the U-side and
D-side block-cell columns generated by admitting divisor `a` before squaring.
At a cell their two coordinates are built from

\[
 z^{U}_{a;n,k}=\mathbf1_{a\le n}\sqrt{w_n}\mu(a)
 (c_{a,k},-\sqrt{q_k}f_{a,k}),
\]
\[
 z^{D}_{a;n,k}=\mathbf1_{a\le n}\sqrt{\beta_n}\mu(a)(\log a)
 (c_{a,k},-\sqrt{q_k}f_{a,k}),                           \tag{42.8}
\]

with the affine U contribution retained in `Y`. Then every entry of `b` is a
signed D-vector/divisor-column correlation, including D--U correlations when
`z_a^U` is used, and every entry of `G` is a divisor-column Gram correlation.
The signs are retained in both objects; no termwise absolute value is needed.
Formula (42.7) says exactly how much of the orthogonal square missing from
(42.4) is recovered by each admitted divisor direction.

A standard dual large-sieve corollary is also available. If a family of probes
satisfies

\[
 \left\|\sum_jc_jz_j\right\|^2\le\Delta\sum_j|c_j|^2,
\]

then

\[
 \boxed{P\ge {1\over\Delta}\sum_j|\langle X,z_j\rangle|^2.} \tag{42.9}
\]

This is the useful orientation of a large sieve here: an upper frame bound for
the divisor probes produces a lower bound for the positive D packet while the
numerators preserve the old--new correlations.

## 4. Exact obstruction and promising target

The scalar route fails because the aggregate U direction is badly aligned with
the D packet; the recovery is mostly orthogonal to U. A sign-blind large sieve
applied directly to Mobius coefficients fails for the complementary reason:
it estimates coefficient magnitude and erases the contractions in `b`.

The multiprobe lemma isolates a narrower arithmetic target. For a selected
set `S(A,B)` of divisor-admission probes, prove

\[
 \boxed{b_{S}^*G_{S}^+b_{S}\ge N}                       \tag{42.10}
\]

on the required complete blocks, or prove the sufficient dual-frame estimate

\[
 \sum_{a\in S(A,B)}|\langle X,z_a\rangle|^2
 \ge \Delta_{A,B}N.                                    \tag{42.11}
\]

Unlike the original packet inequality, (42.11) separates a geometric task
(an upper Gram/frame bound `Delta_(A,B)`) from a Mobius-specific arithmetic
task (a lower bound for the still-signed correlations). The exact obstruction
is that generic Gram positivity supplies the former type of control but no
lower bound for the correlation numerator. Thus (42.6)--(42.11) are a
promising lemma and a precise target, not a proved asymptotic packet comparison
or an RH result.
