## Selector lemma

Let `F_b` be the common exact CNF generated by

```
generate(18, b, 5, robust_witness=True, arc_minimal=True),  b in {6,7}.
```

For distinct five-hole projections `H_1,...,H_t`, introduce selectors
`s_1,...,s_t`, add the at-least-one clause

```
s_1 or ... or s_t,
```

and, for every pair `e` and member `i`, add

```
not s_i or h_e       if e is in H_i,
not s_i or not h_e   otherwise.
```

Then the resulting formula `G` is satisfiable iff at least one fixed parent
`F_b + (h=H_i)` is satisfiable.

*Proof.*  If `G` has a model, its at-least-one clause makes some `s_i` true;
all 153 guarded clauses for that selector then fix the complete hole vector to
`H_i`, so restricting the model gives a model of parent `i`.  Conversely, a
model of parent `i` extends to `G` by setting only `s_i` true.  No
at-most-one clauses are needed: distinct complete projections are already
incompatible if two selectors are true.  This is an equisatisfiable
disjunction, not an inference from separate unretained solver runs. `QED`

## Retained proof artifact versus solver observations

Exactly one pilot proof artifact is retained in this checkpoint.  The batch
`B6-main-P6-00` represents 100 specific B6 parents, has 23,716 variables and
158,037 clauses, and regenerates to CNF SHA-256
`9d551c0a...f58724d1`.  Its 6,685,485-byte LRAT (SHA-256
`0b4e4558...27ecfa`) is retained as a 349,896-byte xz-9 object (SHA-256
`5bb24d65...215f1`).  The strict replay script authenticates the frozen cover
and CNF through the independent structural checker, authenticates and
freshly decompresses the object, authenticates the raw proof, requires the
pinned `lrat-check` binary (SHA-256 `e9e71c96...065b8`), and receives
`c VERIFIED`.  By the selector lemma, this certificate eliminates exactly
those 100 parents.  It does **not** eliminate its 4,255 B6 siblings, any B7
parent, or the m=5 branch.

Everything below is a solver observation only; no additional LRAT from those
runs is retained or promoted.

Batch size has a severe non-monotone proof-order cliff.  For the old P6 prefix,
100 members produced the small proof above, whereas 120 members produced an
891 MB raw proof (about 200 MB with xz-1), and the 688-member formula produced a
1.3 GB raw proof (210 MB with xz-9) after 478 seconds.  Hence “one batch per
shape” is not a compact replacement for the 7.66 GB direct archive.

More importantly, B7 exposes a cover-order defect in the naive 100-row
schedule as a *proof schedule*: `B7-all-P6-00` timed out after 1,200 seconds and
left a 2.9 GB partial LRAT.  Prefixes through parent 19 solve quickly, while
adding parent 20 causes the same timeout even in a singleton.  This is not a
cover defect—the 3,083-row cover and guarded CNFs are exact—but it proves that
index-contiguous batches are not a viable certificate campaign without
adaptive splitting or structural refinement.

## Untouched scope and next exact route

This checkpoint does not:

1. retain or authenticate a complete LRAT ledger (only one 100-parent B6
   pilot is retained and replayed);
2. eliminate the remaining 7,338 of the 7,438 B6/B7 parents merely from local
   solver statuses;
3. validate the upstream theorem classification reducing all five-edge graphs
   to these five types;
4. close the order-18 `m=5` branch; or
5. imply SNC.

The strongest next compression route is deterministic adaptive proof splitting:
start from the exact 80-batch manifest, bisect any batch that exceeds a fixed
resource/proof-size cap, and freeze a split tree whose leaves have retained,
authenticated, replayed LRATs.  The B7 parent-20 observation says the split API
must also permit singleton structural refinements; batching alone cannot solve
a hard parent.  For those singleton leaves, the existing exact C-row and robust
witness hierarchy (with the corrected five two-high witness orbits) remains the
human/finite reduction route.
