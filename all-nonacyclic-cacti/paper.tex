\begin{abstract}
Let $G$ be a connected cactus of cyclomatic rank nine on $n$ vertices.  We
prove $s^+(G)>n$.  The sharp block-additive doubly nonnegative estimate reduces
the problem exactly to $T^8Q$ and $T^7PP$, where $T=C_3$, $P=C_5$, and $Q$ is
an arbitrary cycle.  In the disconnected shared-cut case, exact colored
partition audits give $66=63+3$ and $117=109+8$ proper rows.  The remaining
$T^8Q$ endpoint is settled by a directly verified packing-one theorem.  The
$T^7P\mid P$ endpoint has $3188=3150+38$ marked classes, with sequential
final-owner refinement; the two-interface $P\mid A_7\mid P$ endpoint has
$3188=3182+6$ classes and six explicit residual repairs.  In the fully shared
case an exact $T^8Q$ incidence census leaves only a common-cut bouquet and, for
the hostile ledger, one packing-one two-cut type.  The exact $T^7PP$ census is
$8004=7997+7$; we list all seven replacements, including the leaf-pentagon
opening F9, and give an executable seven-row verifier.  Every separation is at
an actual bridge or along proper cycle intervals, and every connector remnant,
shared cut, incidence branch, and attached tree has exactly one final owner.
The proof expressly uses neither the unproved two-pivot winding assertion nor
the unproved candidate separator.
\end{abstract}

\section{Definitions, provenance, and firewall}

All graphs are finite, simple, and undirected.  A cactus is a connected graph
whose blocks are bridges or cycles.  Its cyclomatic rank is its number of
cyclic blocks.  If the adjacency eigenvalues of $G$ are
$\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$, put
\[
 \spE(G)=\sum_{\lambda_i>0}\lambda_i^2,
 \qquad \smE(G)=\sum_{\lambda_i<0}\lambda_i^2,
 \qquad \sig(G)=\spE(G)-|V(G)|.
\]
For an induced vertex partition $V(G)=V_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}\cdots
\mathbin{\dot\cup}V_r$, positive square energy is superadditive:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:super}
 \spE(G)\geq\sum_i\spE(G[V_i]),
 \qquad \sig(G)\geq\sum_i\sig(G[V_i]).
\end{equation}
This is the theorem of Akbari, Kumar, Mohar, and Pragada~\cite{AKMP}.
Since a rank-nine connected graph has $|E|=n+8$, our theorem proves, strictly,
the conjectured inequality in~\cite[Conjecture~1.2]{AKMPZ} for this class.

Write $T=C_3$, $P=C_5$, and
\[
 \delta=\sec(\pi/5)-1=\sqrt5-2<\frac14,
 \qquad \delta_q=\sec(\pi/q)-1<1.
\]
A cycle $Q=C_q$ is \emph{hostile} when $q\equiv1\pmod4$.

The controlling synthesis is~\cite{Synthesis}, whose dependency boundary was
independently subjected to the hostile audit~\cite{HostileAudit}.  We use the
corrected octacyclic infrastructure~\cite{Octacyclic}, the exact rank-nine DNN
calculation~\cite{DNNResidual}, the disconnected marked-interface certificates
\cite{Disconnected,TwoInterface}, the fully shared census~\cite{SharedCensus},
and the seven-row replacement verifier~\cite{Seven}.

Two tempting statements are outside the proof.  We do \emph{not} use the
two-pivot phase or winding assertion discussed in~\cite{TwoPivot}: its exact
two-terminal reduction does not prove the required integrated argument bound.
We also do \emph{not} use the census-free candidate separator from the
rank-uniform router note~\cite{CandidateSeparator}.  Every two-interface core
below is instead discharged by a finite checked router certificate or an
explicit pentagon opening.  No principal-$\arctan$ shortcut, hidden two-pivot
estimate, edge monotonicity, connector contraction, or unproved Voronoi guard
is imported.

\section{Packet bounds and induced ownership}

We use the established bounds, uniformly over arbitrary finite trees attached
at arbitrary packet vertices,
\begin{align}
 \sig(H)&\geq0 &&\text{for connected ranks two and three},\label{eq:low0}\\
 \sig(H)&>0 &&\text{for connected ranks four through eight},\label{eq:lowpos}\\
 \sig(P)&\geq-\delta,& \sig(Q)&\geq-\delta_q
   &&\text{for hostile $Q$},\label{eq:hostile}\\
 \sig(TP)&>1-\delta,& \sig(TQ)&>0,\label{eq:mixed}\\
 \sig(PP)&>0,& \sig(TPP)&>\frac32.\label{eq:pp}
\end{align}
The authoritative octacyclic manuscript assembles the rank bounds and packet
provenance~\cite[Section~2]{Octacyclic}.  Their sources are the rank-two through
rank-eight cactus theorems
\cite{Bicyclic,Tricyclic,Tetracyclic,Pentacyclic,Hexacyclic,Heptacyclic,Octacyclic}.
For one connected shared-cut cluster $A_r$ of $r$ triangles,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triangle}
 \sig(A_r)>b_r,
 \qquad (b_1,\ldots,b_7)=(0,1,2,3,2,1,0).
\end{equation}
The corrected matching-injection proof and recurrence, not a
multiplicity-blind Sturm count, are cited in~\cite[Section~2]{Octacyclic}.

We need two analytic packet theorems.

\begin{lemma}[One-hostile-cycle packing one]\label{lem:packing}
Suppose the cyclic blocks of a connected cactus $H$ are one hostile $Q=C_q$
and $a\geq1$ triangles, no two of which are vertex-disjoint.  The $Q$ may meet
the triangular part at its root or be joined to that root by an arbitrary
positive path.  Arbitrary trees may occur everywhere, including on the path.
Then
\[
 \spE(H)-\smE(H)>-2\delta_q,
 \qquad \sig(H)>a-\delta_q.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Eliminate every tree off the cyclic spine using its signless matching message
$M_{u\to v}(t)=t+\sum_wM_{w\to u}(t)^{-1}\geq t$.  This replaces spine
activities by $\alpha_v=t+y_v\geq t$ and removes a positive real factor.  With
$S=V(Q)$, the grouped Sachs expansion is
\[
 \Psi_H(t)=R+2i(B-A),
\]
where $B=Z_{H-S}(\alpha)$, $A=\sum_TZ_{H-V(T)}(\alpha)$, and
\[
 R=Z_H(\alpha)+4\sum_{T\cap Q=\varnothing}
 Z_{H-(S\cup V(T))}(\alpha)>0.
\]
Packing one is used exactly to exclude terms containing two triangles.  If
$Z_q(t)$ is the bare matching partition of $Q$, splitting matchings by boundary
use gives $Z_H=Z_Q(\alpha|_S)B+E$, $E\geq0$, and
$Z_Q(\alpha|_S)=Z_q+L$, $L\geq0$.  Hence
\[
 R-Z_q(B-A)=E+LB+Z_qA+4\sum_{T\cap Q=\varnothing}
 Z_{H-(S\cup V(T))}(\alpha)>0.
\]
Thus the continuous argument of $\Psi_H$ is strictly below the isolated-$Q$
argument.  The signed Coulson identity and
$\spE(Q)-\smE(Q)=-2\delta_q$ prove the first claim.  Since
$|E(H)|=|V(H)|+a$ and $\spE+\smE=2|E|$, the second follows.  This is the
standalone proof of~\cite{PackingOne}; it has no all-rank decomposition premise.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Common-cut Schur--Sachs bounds]\label{lem:common}
For a one-vertex bouquet, uniformly over arbitrary attached trees,
\begin{align}
 \sig(T^kQ)&>k-\delta_q &&(q\equiv1\pmod4),\label{eq:ccq}\\
 \sig(T^kQ)&>k &&(q\text{ even or }q\equiv3\pmod4),\label{eq:ccgood}\\
 \sig(T^kP)&>k-\delta,\label{eq:ccp}\\
 \sig(T^kPP)&>k+1-\frac4{3\sqrt{13}}.\label{eq:ccpp}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After exact tree elimination, let $A_j$ be the positive matching partition of
the private path of lobe $j$ and $B_j$ the positive contribution obtained by
matching the common pivot into that lobe.  The normalized determinant is the
scalar expression
\[
 W(t)=a_x+\sum_j\frac{B_j+\omega_{\ell_j}}{A_j},
 \qquad \omega_\ell=-2i^{-\ell}.
\]
For $T^kQ$, $A_QX>Z_q(t)$, where
$X=a_x+B_Q/A_Q+\sum B_j/A_j>0$.  The positive imaginary phase, if any, is
therefore strictly below that of isolated $Q$; in the other congruence classes
the phase is negative.  Coulson integration gives~\eqref{eq:ccq} and
\eqref{eq:ccgood}, and $Q=P$ gives~\eqref{eq:ccp}.

For two pentagons, adding triangles strictly increases the positive real part
and decreases the imaginary part.  The exact coefficient inequality
\[
 2R\geq t(t^4+7t^2+9)(A_1+A_2)
\]
and
$\int_0^\infty4/(t^4+7t^2+9)\,dt=2\pi/(3\sqrt{13})$
give~\eqref{eq:ccpp}.  The full scalar proof and the 1290-term coefficient
certificate are~\cite{CommonCut,Coefficient}.  This lemma is invoked only when
all named cycles really share one pivot.
\end{proof}

Join cyclic blocks when they share a cut; the components are \emph{shared-cut
clusters}.  Contracting each cluster while retaining actual bridge paths gives
a tree.  We repeatedly use the following ownership fact, proved in the
octacyclic infrastructure~\cite[Lemmas~2.3--2.4]{Octacyclic}.

\begin{lemma}[Territory realization]\label{lem:ownership}
Connected subtrees assigned in the reduced cluster tree can be realized as
connected induced territories by cutting actual bridges.  If a selected cycle
has two or three distinct cyclic marks, assigning the marks to nonempty proper
consecutive intervals realizes connected induced territories; coincident marks
remain one group.  A later split refines one existing territory.  Every
connector remnant, shared cut, incidence branch, and off-hull tree follows its
unique mark or hull attachment and has exactly one final owner.
\end{lemma}

\section{Exact DNN reduction}

For an edge-containing graph set
\[
 \kappa(G)=\sup_{\substack{M\succeq0,\ M\geq0\\\one^TM\one>0}}
 \frac{4(\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\sqrt{M_{uv}})^2}{\one^TM\one}.
\]
The sharp cactus DNN theorem~\cite{DNN,LTZ} says
\[
 \kappa(G)=b+\sum_i\kappa(C_{\ell_i}),\qquad
 \kappa(C_\ell)=\begin{cases}
 \ell,&\ell\text{ even},\\
 \ell\sec^2(\pi/(2\ell)),&\ell\text{ odd},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \smE(G)\leq\kappa(G).
\]
Put $\epsilon_\ell=0$ for even $\ell$ and
$\epsilon_\ell=\ell\tan^2(\pi/(2\ell))$ for odd $\ell$.  Block counting and
$|E|=n+8$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dnn}
 \sig(G)\geq8-\sum_{i=1}^9\epsilon_{\ell_i}.
\end{equation}
The odd sequence decreases, $\epsilon_3=1$, and, for
$a=\epsilon_5=5-2\sqrt5$,
\[
 3a<2,\qquad2a>1,\qquad\epsilon_5+\epsilon_7<1.
\]
These comparisons have exact rational/squaring certificates in
\cite{DNNResidual}.  At most six triangles gives
$\sum\epsilon_i\leq6+3a<8$.  With seven triangles, only two pentagons can
contribute at least one.  With at least eight triangles the multiset is
$T^8Q$, allowing $Q=T$.  Thus the complete and exact residual list is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residuals}
 T^8Q=\{3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3,q\},\qquad
 T^7PP=\{3,3,3,3,3,3,3,5,5\}.
\end{equation}
Every other rank-nine cactus is strict directly from~\eqref{eq:dnn}.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$A_8\mid Q$}{A8 | Q} endpoint}

Mark the first cyclic-hull entry of the connector to $Q$.  If the
eight-triangle incidence is not locked at one common cut, choose an internal
triangle router and include a private connector entry among its at most three
marks.  Split it into proper intervals.  If the $Q$ territory retains $k$
triangles, then $k=1$ is strict by $TQ$; $k=2$ is nonnegative with another
strict triangular branch; and $3\leq k\leq7$ is strict by the lower-rank
theorem.  If $k=0$, the other seven triangles occupy at most two branches, so
one contains at least four triangles and has surplus $>3$, absorbing
$\sig(Q)>-1$.

If no such router exists, all eight triangles contain one hub.  Their packing
number is one even when the connector enters through a private bouquet vertex.
Lemma~\ref{lem:packing} applies to the actual joining path and gives
\[
 \sig(G)>8-\delta_q>0.
\]
Nonhostile $Q$ is easier by the bridge packet ledger.  This proves every
disconnected $T^8Q$ cactus and supplies the structural closure behind
$66=63+3$; no two-pivot statement is involved.

\section{Main theorem and exhaustion}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Every connected cactus $G$ of cyclomatic rank nine, on $n$ vertices, satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\spE(G)>n}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The exact DNN inequality~\eqref{eq:dnn} proves every cycle multiset except
\eqref{eq:residuals}.  For a residual graph, the reduced shared-cut cluster
graph either has more than one cluster or consists of one fully shared cluster.
In the first case, the exact $66=63+3$ and $117=109+8$ audits reduce all rows
to direct packet ledgers or the three endpoints~\eqref{eq:endpoints}.  The
$A_8\mid Q$ router/packing-one proof, the $3188=3150+38$ entry-locked
certificate with final-owner refinement, and the $3188=3182+6$ two-interface
certificate prove those endpoints.  In the second case, the complete $T^8Q$
census and its two analytic closures prove every incidence, while the complete
$8004=7997+7$ census and the seven explicit replacements prove every $T^7PP$
incidence.

Every global separation is made at an actual bridge.  Every cycle sacrifice
uses nonempty proper consecutive intervals.  Later splits refine one induced
owner, and final-owner replacement assigns every shared cut, connector remnant,
incidence branch, and attached tree exactly once.  The two pentagon openings
record their exact nonempty-tree cost $-1$.  Common-cut estimates are used only
at a real common pivot, and packing one only after all relevant triangles are
directly seen to contain one hub.  No unproved two-pivot winding statement and
no candidate separator occurs.  Thus every case has $\sig(G)>0$.
\end{proof}
