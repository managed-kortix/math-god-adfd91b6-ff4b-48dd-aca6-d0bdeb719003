\begin{abstract}
Let $G$ be a connected cactus of cyclomatic rank ten on $n$ vertices.  We
prove $s^+(G)>n$.  The sharp block-additive doubly nonnegative estimate leaves
exactly $T^9Q$ and $T^8PP$, where $T=C_3$, $P=C_5$, and $Q$ is an arbitrary
cycle.  In the disconnected shared-cut case, exact colored-partition audits
give $96=92+4$ and $180=170+10$ proper rows.  The only endpoint kernels have
the exact closures $A_9\mid Q$: $3624=3618+6$, entry-locked
$T^8P\mid P$: $11689=11586+100+3$, and $P\mid A_8\mid P$:
$11689=11674+15$.  In the fully shared case the complete stabilized $T^9Q$
census has $8049$ types and three hostile exceptions.  The complete $T^8PP$
census is $30386=30377+9$; all nine replacements are verified, including all
$5\cdot4\cdot3=60$ cyclic placements in the pentagon-router case N4.  Global
cuts are actual bridges, local cuts are proper cycle intervals, later cuts
refine one owner, and arbitrary attached trees follow their unique final hull
owner.  The proof uses neither a separator theorem nor a two-pivot phase or
winding assertion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Definitions, inputs, and proof firewall}

All graphs are finite, simple, and undirected.  A cactus is a connected graph
whose blocks are bridges or cycles; its cyclomatic rank is the number of cyclic
blocks.  If the adjacency eigenvalues of $G$ are
$\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$, put
\[
 \spE(G)=\sum_{\lambda_i>0}\lambda_i^2,
 \qquad \smE(G)=\sum_{\lambda_i<0}\lambda_i^2,
 \qquad \sig(G)=\spE(G)-|V(G)|.
\]
For an induced vertex partition
$V(G)=V_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}\cdots\mathbin{\dot\cup}V_r$, positive square
energy is superadditive~\cite{AKMP}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:super}
 \spE(G)\geq\sum_i\spE(G[V_i]),
 \qquad \sig(G)\geq\sum_i\sig(G[V_i]).
\end{equation}
A connected rank-ten graph has $|E|=n+9$, so the main theorem proves strictly
the conjectured lower bound of~\cite{AKMPZ} for this class.

Write $T=C_3$, $P=C_5$, and
\[
 \delta=\sec(\pi/5)-1=\sqrt5-2<\frac14,
 \qquad \delta_q=\sec(\pi/q)-1<1.
\]
A cycle $Q=C_q$ is \emph{hostile} when $q\equiv1\pmod4$.

The dependency boundary is the corrected synthesis~\cite{Synthesis}, accepted
by the hostile audit~\cite{HostileAudit}.  We use the authoritative corrected
octacyclic infrastructure~\cite{Octacyclic}, the proved rank-nine
theorem~\cite{Nonacyclic}, the exact sharp-DNN frontier~\cite{DNNResidual}, and
the finite certificates cited at each census.  Those references are proof
packets, not numerical evidence: they supply the analytic inequalities or
finite exhaustive checks stated here.

Two statements are expressly outside the proof.  We do not use the open
two-pivot Schur--Sachs phase/winding assertion~\cite{TwoPivot}; its exact
two-terminal determinant state does not imply the required integrated phase
bound.  We do not use candidate separator Lemma S or any census-free
``good-router'' assertion~\cite{CandidateSeparator}.  No connector is
contracted, no edge-monotonicity or principal-$\arctan$ shortcut is used, and
no two-interface row is inferred from an unproved Voronoi rule.

\section{Packet bounds, intervals, and ownership}

The rank-two through rank-nine cactus theorems and the corrected octacyclic
packet infrastructure give, uniformly over arbitrary finite trees attached at
arbitrary packet vertices,
\begin{align}
 \sig(H)&\geq0 &&\text{for connected ranks two and three},\label{eq:low0}\\
 \sig(H)&>0 &&\text{for connected ranks four through nine},\label{eq:lowpos}\\
 \sig(P)&\geq-\delta,& \sig(Q)&\geq-\delta_q
   &&\text{for hostile $Q$},\label{eq:hostile}\\
 \sig(TP)&>\frac34,& \sig(TQ)&>0,\label{eq:mixed}\\
 \sig(PP)&>0,& \sig(TPP)&>\frac32
   &&\text{for one shared-cut cluster}.\label{eq:pp}
\end{align}
The strict $PP$ estimate in~\eqref{eq:pp} has precisely the displayed scope:
the two pentagons belong to one connected shared-cut cluster, with arbitrary
finite trees attached at its vertices.  This is the corrected packet theorem
of~\cite[Eq.~(2.5)]{Octacyclic}; no claim is made here for an arbitrary
two-pentagon cactus joined by a positive-length bridge.
Here and below a packet name permits arbitrary joining paths and rooted trees
within the scope of the cited packet theorem.  For one connected shared-cut
cluster $A_r$ of $r$ triangles,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triangle}
 \sig(A_r)>b_r,
 \qquad (b_1,\ldots,b_9)=(0,1,2,3,2,1,0,0,0).
\end{equation}
The entries through $r=8$ are the corrected triangular-cluster bounds in
\cite{Octacyclic}; $r=9$ uses~\cite{Nonacyclic}.  Only strict positivity is
used at $r=8,9$.

Two analytic packets close the locked one-pivot rows.

\begin{lemma}[Rooted packing one]\label{lem:packing}
Let a connected cactus $H$ contain one hostile cycle $Q=C_q$ and $a\geq1$
triangles, no two vertex-disjoint.  The cycle $Q$ may meet their common root or
be joined to it by an arbitrary path.  Arbitrary trees may be attached to the
cycles, joining path, and its vertices.  Then
\[
 \spE(H)-\smE(H)>-2\delta_q,
 \qquad \sig(H)>a-\delta_q.
\]
The same statement with $Q=P$ gives $\sig(H)>a-\delta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the rooted packing-one theorem of~\cite{PackingOne}.  Briefly, exact
tree elimination replaces each spine activity by a positive matching message
$\alpha_v\geq t$.  Grouping the Sachs expansion by the unique hostile cycle
gives $\Psi_H(t)=R+2i(B-A)$ with $R>0$.  Packing one excludes products of two
triangle terms, and splitting matchings by boundary use gives
\[
 R-Z_q(t)(B-A)=E+LB+Z_q(t)A+
 4\sum_{T\cap Q=\varnothing}Z_{H-(V(Q)\cup V(T))}(\alpha)>0.
\]
Thus the continuous argument is strictly smaller than the isolated hostile
cycle argument.  The signed Coulson identity gives the first inequality, and
$\spE+\smE=2|E|=2(|V|+a)$ gives the second.  The message proof already includes
arbitrary rooted trees and arbitrary joining-path length.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Common-cut Schur--Sachs packets]\label{lem:common}
If all named cycles share one actual cut vertex, uniformly over arbitrary
attached trees,
\begin{align}
 \sig(T^kQ)&>k-\delta_q &&(q\equiv1\pmod4),\label{eq:ccq}\\
 \sig(T^kQ)&>k &&(q\text{ even or }q\equiv3\pmod4),\label{eq:ccgood}\\
 \sig(T^kP)&>k-\delta,\label{eq:ccp}\\
 \sig(T^kPP)&>k+1-\frac4{3\sqrt{13}}.\label{eq:ccpp}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After exact tree elimination, the normalized determinant is the scalar state
\[
 W(t)=a_x+\sum_j\frac{B_j+\omega_{\ell_j}}{A_j},
 \qquad \omega_\ell=-2i^{-\ell},
\]
with $A_j,B_j,a_x>0$.  The one-hostile-cycle phase is bounded strictly by the
isolated-cycle phase.  For two pentagons the exact coefficient inequality
$2R\geq t(t^4+7t^2+9)(A_1+A_2)$ and
$\int_0^\infty4/(t^4+7t^2+9)\,dt=2\pi/(3\sqrt{13})$ give the final line.
The full argument and its 1290-term exact coefficient certificate are
\cite{CommonCut,Coefficient}.  This scalar lemma is invoked only at one real
common pivot; it is not a two-pivot estimate.
\end{proof}

We now state explicitly the graph-realization facts that connect the finite
incidence certificates to induced partitions.

\begin{lemma}[Proper cycle intervals]\label{lem:interval}
Let $C$ be a cyclic block carrying two or three distinct marked vertices.  For
two marks, one mark and the complementary two-vertex interval on a triangle,
or either nonempty proper consecutive arc on a longer cycle, are connected
induced intervals.  Three marks on a triangle give three singleton intervals.
For the N4 pentagon router, isolating one marked vertex leaves a four-vertex
induced path containing the other two marks, for every injection of the three
marks into the five cycle vertices.  Incidence branches attached at a mark may
be assigned to that mark's interval.  If a later router lies in one interval,
splitting it refines only that interval and preserves an induced partition.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Deleting the boundary edges between consecutive arcs of a chordless cycle
leaves induced paths (or singletons).  Distinct triangle marks have cyclic gaps
$1+2$ or $1+1+1$.  On $C_5$, deleting the isolated marked vertex leaves the
unique complementary four-vertex path, independent of the cyclic placement of
the other two marks.  Attached cactus branches meet the cycle at only their
marked cut, so adjoining a branch preserves connectedness and inducedness.
Refinement replaces one part by an induced partition of that part.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Final-owner realization]\label{lem:ownership}
Contract maximal shared-cut cyclic clusters while retaining every actual
bridge and every internal connector vertex.  The resulting cluster graph is a
tree.  A partition of it into connected subtrees, together with legal interval
splits from Lemma~\ref{lem:interval}, realizes connected, induced, disjoint,
exhaustive territories in $G$.  Every shared cut, connector remnant, incidence
branch, cycle interval, and off-hull tree has exactly one final owner.  A later
split replaces the provisional owner of one active territory; no provisional
owner may survive.  A private interval containing no retained cycle is a
nonempty tree and has exact surplus $-1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The block-cut graph of a cactus is a tree.  Contracting connected subtrees of
cyclic blocks cannot create a cycle, and retaining bridge paths preserves all
vertices.  Cutting an actual bridge separates two connected induced vertex
sets.  At a cyclic router, Lemma~\ref{lem:interval} assigns each vertex and
each attached incidence branch once.  Induct on the sequence of refinements;
the active territory being split is replaced by disjoint exhaustive induced
subterritories, while all other owners remain unchanged.  An off-hull tree has
a unique first hull attachment and follows its final owner.  Finally, for a
nonempty tree $E$, bipartite spectral symmetry and $|E(E)|=|V(E)|-1$ give
$\spE(E)=|V(E)|-1$ and hence $\sig(E)=-1$.
\end{proof}

These lemmas are the formal ownership and interval interface of the corrected
octacyclic infrastructure~\cite{Octacyclic}.  They cover arbitrary connector
lengths, branching connector trees, coincident interfaces, and arbitrary
finite trees at every core or connector vertex.

\section{Exact sharp-DNN reduction}

For an edge-containing graph define
\[
 \kappa(G)=\sup_{\substack{M\succeq0,\ M\geq0\\\one^TM\one>0}}
 \frac{4(\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\sqrt{M_{uv}})^2}{\one^TM\one}.
\]
The sharp cactus DNN theorem~\cite{DNN,LTZ} says
\[
 \kappa(G)=b+\sum_i\kappa(C_{\ell_i}),\qquad
 \kappa(C_\ell)=\begin{cases}
 \ell,&\ell\text{ even},\\
 \ell\sec^2(\pi/(2\ell)),&\ell\text{ odd},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \smE(G)\leq\kappa(G).
\]
Put $\epsilon_\ell=0$ for even $\ell$ and
$\epsilon_\ell=\ell\tan^2(\pi/(2\ell))$ for odd $\ell$.  If the ten cyclic
blocks have lengths $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_{10}$, block counting and $|E|=n+9$
give the exact bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dnn}
 \sig(G)\geq9-\sum_{i=1}^{10}\epsilon_{\ell_i}.
\end{equation}
The odd sequence decreases strictly.  With
$a=\epsilon_5=5-2\sqrt5$,
\[
 \epsilon_3=1,\qquad 3a<2,\qquad2a>1,
 \qquad\epsilon_5+\epsilon_7<1.
\]
The first two comparisons reduce after squaring positive sides to $169<180$
and $80<81$; the last follows from
$\epsilon_7<7/15<2\sqrt5-4$, with $4489<4500$.  These exact certificates and
monotonicity are recorded in~\cite{DNNResidual}.

If at most seven cycles are triangles, then
$\sum\epsilon_i\leq7+3a<9$.  With exactly eight triangles, the two remaining
cycles contribute at least one only when both are pentagons: an even cycle
contributes zero, and every other odd pair contributes at most
$\epsilon_5+\epsilon_7<1$.  At least nine triangles has the form $T^9Q$,
including $Q=T$.  Consequently the exact, disjoint DNN residual frontier is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residuals}
 T^9Q=\{3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3,q\},\ q\geq3,
 \qquad T^8PP=\{3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3,5,5\}.
\end{equation}
Every other rank-ten cycle multiset is strict directly from~\eqref{eq:dnn}.

activity and nesting, proper interval sizes, all N4 cyclic placements, packet
connectivity, common-cut or packing-one hypotheses, cycle coverage, connector

Thus $30386=30377+9$ is a complete closure.

\section{Main theorem and exhaustion}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Every connected cactus $G$ of cyclomatic rank ten, on $n$ vertices, satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\spE(G)>n}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The exact DNN inequality~\eqref{eq:dnn} proves every cycle multiset except the
two disjoint residual families~\eqref{eq:residuals}.  For a residual cactus,
the reduced shared-cut cluster tree has one node or more than one.

With more than one node, the exact audits contain 96 proper $T^9Q$ rows,
partitioned as $96=92+4$, and 180 proper $T^8PP$ rows, partitioned as
$180=170+10$.  The direct rows have valid packet ledgers.  Actual reduced-tree
leaf/path moves reduce the structural rows exactly to
$A_9\mid Q$, $T^8P\mid P$, and $P\mid A_8\mid P$.  Their exhaustive marked
closures are respectively $3624=3618+6$, $11689=11586+100+3$, and
$11689=11674+15$.

With one node, the complete capacity-dependent $T^9Q$ census and its three
hostile replacements close every incidence.  The complete $T^8PP$ census has
$30386=30377+9$ rows, and the nine listed replacements close every exception,
including all N4 placements.

Lemma~\ref{lem:ownership} upgrades each incidence certificate to connected
induced territories.  Every global cut is an actual bridge; every local split
is a nonempty proper cycle interval; every later split refines one owner; and
every shared cut, connector remnant, incidence branch, interval, opened vertex,
and attached tree is assigned once.  Common-cut bounds are used only at one
actual pivot and packing one only when all retained triangles contain the
displayed hub.  Each opening records its exact nonempty-tree cost.  No
separator or two-pivot assertion occurs.  Equation~\eqref{eq:super} therefore
gives $\sig(G)>0$ in every case.
\end{proof}
